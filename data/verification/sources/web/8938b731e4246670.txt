 Arabic
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Classical Persian مَاسْت ( māst ) , from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( m’st' /⁠māst⁠/ , “ yoghurt ” ) , ultimately from Classical Syriac ܡܣܬܐ ( mastā ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 مَاسْت • ( māst )  m 

 ( obsolete ) yoghurt 
 a. 1222 , نَجِيب الدِّين السَّمَرْقَنْدِيّ [ najīb ad-dīn as-samarqandiyy ], edited by Juliane Müller, كِتَاب الْأَغْذِيَة وَٱلْأَشْرِبَة [ kitāb al-ʔaḡdiya wa-l-ʔǎšriba ] (Islamic Philosophy, Theology and Science. Texts and Studies; 101 )‎ [1] , Leiden: Brill, published 2017 , →ISBN , page 120 : الماست أبرد من اللبن الحليب يطفّئ الحرارة ويصلح أن يؤكل قبل الطعام في يوم تعب وحرّ. Yoghurt is colder than fresh milk, extinguishes the heat and is aptly eaten before a meal on a hard and hot day.

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Classical Persian مَاسْت ( māst ) , from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( m’st' /⁠māst⁠/ , “ yoghurt ” ) , ultimately from Classical Syriac ܡܣܬܐ ( mastā ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 ماست • ( mast ) ( definite accusative ماستی ( mastı ) , plural ماستلر ( mastlar ) ) ( literary ) 

 yogurt , a milk -based product stiffened by a bacterium -aided curdling process
 Synonyms: ثمیل ( semil ) ( literary ) , لبن ( leben ) ( literary ) , یوغورت ( yoğurt )

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( m’st' /⁠māst⁠/ , “ yoghurt ” ) , from Classical Syriac ܡܣܬܐ ( mastā ) . 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 ماست • ( māst / mâst ) ( plural ماست‌ها ( māst-hā / mâst-hâ ) , Tajik spelling мост ) 

 yoghurt 
 ماست و خیار ― māst u xiyār  / mâst o xiyâr ― mast-o-khiyar , a cucumber and yoghurt side dish
