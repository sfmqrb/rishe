 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Originally * “ spice, garnish ” , from a similar Middle Iranian source as Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( ʾp̄cʾl, ʾp̄zʾl /⁠abzār⁠/ , “ instrument, means; spice ” ) and the Old Armenian borrowing աւճառ, աճառ, օճառ ( awčaṙ, ačaṙ, ōčaṙ ) , most likely from a Proto-Iranian compound *Habí-čārna- ( literally “ making/remedying around ” ) , from *Habí- (from Proto-Indo-Iranian *Habʰí ) + the root of *čārah ( “ remedy, means ” ) (compare Avestan 𐬗𐬁𐬭𐬁 ( cārā ) , Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( cʾl /⁠čār⁠/ ) , modern Persian چار ( čâr ) , Old Armenian ճար ( čar , “ remedy, means ” ) ), ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *h₂m̥bʰí ( “ around, on both sides ” ) and *kʷer- ( “ to make, produce, do ” ) . Doublet of ابزار ( abzâr , “ tool, instrument, implement, means ” ) . Cognate to Pashto اښار ( āẍār , “ acidic; sour; pickle; sadman ” ) ; and related to dialectal Armenian ավճոր ( avčor , “ garnish ” ) and dialectal Turkish avcar ( “ spices; taste, flavor ” ) . However, many details remain disputed. More at Old Armenian օճառ ( ōčaṙ ) . This etymology is incomplete. You can help Wiktionary by elaborating on the origins of this term.
