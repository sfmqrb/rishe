 English
 [ edit ] 

 A caique (trading vessel) 
 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 IPA ( key ) : /kɑːˈiːk/ , /kaɪˈiːk/ 

 Rhymes: -iːk 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from French caïque , from Italian caicco , from Ottoman Turkish قایق ( kayık ) , [ 1 ] [ 2 ] from Proto-Turkic *kiayguk ( “ boat, oar ” ) . Cognate with modern Turkish kayık .

 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 caïc , caicco , caieque , caïque , kaik , kaiki , kajik , kayik , saique 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 caique ( plural caiques ) 

 ( nautical ) A small wooden trading vessel, brightly painted and rigged for sail , traditionally used for fishing and trawling .
 1950 July, J. C. Mertens, “By the "Taurus Express" to Baghdad”, in Railway Magazine , page 435 : Shipping of every sort, from passenger liners to ferry steamers, tramps to tugs and trailing barges, feluccas to speedboats and yachts, from warships to caiques , chugs, hoots, glides or churns its way in all directions.

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 A caique (parrot) 
 From Spanish caíque or Portuguese caíque .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 caique ( plural caiques ) 

 Any of four (previously two) species of parrot in the genus Pionites .
