 Origin and history of eternity 

 eternity (n.) 

 late 14c., "quality of being eternal," from Old French eternité "eternity, perpetuity" (12c.), from Latin aeternitatem (nominative aeternitas ), from aeternus "enduring, permanent," contraction of aeviternus "of great age," from aevum "age" (from PIE root *aiw- "vital force, life; long life, eternity"). Meaning "infinite time" is from 1580s. In the Mercian hymns, Latin aeternum is glossed by Old English ecnisse .
 also from late 14c. 

 Entries linking to eternity 

 *aiw- 

 also *ayu- , Proto-Indo-European root meaning "vital force, life; long life, eternity."

 It might form all or part of: age ; aught (n.1) "something; anything;" aye (adv.) "always, ever;" Ayurvedic ; coetaneous ; coeval ; each ; eon ; eternal ; eternity ; ever ; every ; ewigkeit ; hygiene ; longevity ; medieval ; nay ; never ; no ; primeval ; sempiternal ; tarnation ; utopia . 

 It might also be the source of: Sanskrit ayu- "life;" Avestan aiiu "age, life(time);" Greek aiōn "age, vital force; a period of existence, a lifetime, a generation; a long space of time," in plural, "eternity;" Latin aevum "space of time, eternity;" Gothic aiws "age, eternity," Old Norse ævi "lifetime," German ewig "everlasting," Old English a "ever, always."
