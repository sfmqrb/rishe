 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Hellenic *tūrós (whence also Mycenaean Greek 𐀶𐀫 ( tu-ro /⁠tūrós⁠/ ) ), probably from Proto-Indo-European *tuh₂-ró-s , from *tewh₂- ( “ to swell ” ) . However, compare Old English ġe-þweor ( “ curds ” ) , from *(s)twerH- ( “ to swirl, churn ” ) . Possibly a formation in common with Proto-Slavic *tvarogъ ( “ quark, whey-cheese ” ) and Avestan 𐬙𐬏𐬌𐬭𐬌 ( tū i ri ) , but the root and morphological details are disputed.

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 τῡρός • ( tūrós )  m ( genitive τῡροῦ ) ; second declension 

 cheese 
 125 CE – 180 CE , Lucian , A True Story 1.24: Greek , English [ … ] κἀπειδὰν ἢ πονῶσιν ἢ γυμνάζωνται, γάλακτι πᾶν τὸ σῶμα ἱδροῦσιν, ὥστε καὶ τυροὺς ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ πήγνυνται, ὀλίγον τοῦ μέλιτος ἐπιστάξαντες· [ … ] [ … ] kapeidàn ḕ ponôsin ḕ gumnázōntai, gálakti pân tò sôma hidroûsin, hṓste kaì turoùs ap’ autoû pḗgnuntai, olígon toû mélitos epistáxantes; [ … ] 1894 translation by Francis Hickes When they labour or exercise themselves, they anoint their body with milk, whereinto if a little of that honey chance to drop, it will be turned into cheese .
