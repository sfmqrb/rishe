 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 σκῐ́φος ( skĭ́phos ) — Aeolic 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Already in Late Mycenaean Greek (Ta-716 from Pylos), attested in the dual 𐀥𐀯𐀟𐀁 ( qi-si-pe-e , “ two swords ” ) (mostly ideographically as 𐃉 ). Probably of Pre-Greek origin, [ 1 ] related to and perhaps borrowed from Egyptian zft ( “ sword, knife ” ) . [ 2 ] If the Greek isn't borrowed directly from the Egyptian, perhaps both are from a Semitic word related to Ge'ez ሰይፍ ( säyf ) , Arabic سَيْف ( sayf ) , and Hebrew סַיִף ( sayip̄ ) [ 3 ] or from a Libyan or " Sea Peoples " word. [ 4 ]

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 ξῐ́φος • ( xĭ́phos )  n ( genitive ξῐ́φεος or ξῐ́φους or ξῐ́φευς ) ; third declension 

 sword ( the short, straight, double-edged sword of the Iron Age and Classical Antiquity ) 
 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Iliad 1.194 

 the sword-shaped bone of the cuttlefish 
 384 BCE – 322 BCE , Aristotle , History of Animals 4.1.21 

 swordfish 
 384 BCE – 322 BCE , Aristotle , Fragments 306

 corn-flag ( Gladiolus italicus )
 Synonym: ξῐ́φῐον ( xĭ́phĭon ) 
 371 BCE – 287 BCE , Theophrastus , Historia Plantarum 7.13.1
