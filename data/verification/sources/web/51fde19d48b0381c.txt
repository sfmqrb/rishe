 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 nūbēs (a cloud) 
 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 nūbis ( post-classical , Vulgar Latin ) 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Italic *nouðetis , from Proto-Indo-European *(s)newdʰ-e-ti-s , from *(s)newdʰ- ( “ to cover ” ) . Cognate with Welsh nudd ( “ haze ” ) , Avestan 𐬯𐬥𐬀𐬊𐬜𐬀 ( snaoδa , “ clouds ” ) , Baluchi [script needed] ( nod , “ raincloud ” ) . [ 1 ] Note that despite similar pronunciation and semantics, not related to nebula ( “ mist, fog ” ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈnuː.beːs] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈnuː.bes] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 nūbēs   f ( genitive nūbis ) ; third declension 

 cloud 
 c. 99 BCE – 55 BCE , Lucretius , De rerum natura 6.156–157 : Denique saepe geli multus fragor atque ruina
grandinis in magnis sonitum dat nubibus alte; Translation by William Ellery Leonard And down-pour of swift hail gives forth a sound
 Among the mighty clouds on high; [ … ] 

 swarm , multitude 
 Synonyms: multitūdō , mōlēs , frequentia , acervus , ūbertās , cōpia 

 phantom , something insubstantial 

 cloudiness , gloominess 

 concealment , obscurity 
 Synonym: obscūritās 

 ( figuratively ) storm-cloud , the appearance of a coming danger

 bad luck , misfortune 
 Synonyms: plāga , īnfortūnium , calamitās , cāsus , miseria , vulnus , malum , cruciātus
