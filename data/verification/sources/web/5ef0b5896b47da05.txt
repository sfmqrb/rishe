 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [script needed] ( spūrdan, spar- /⁠spwltn', spl-⁠/ , “ to tread , trample ” ) [ 1 ] 

 This etymology is missing or incomplete. Please add to it, or discuss it at the Etymology scriptorium . 
Particularly: “Feels like this might be from Proto-Indo-European *sperH- ( “ to kick ” ) . See also Prakrit 𑀨𑀺𑀭𑀇 ( phiraï , “ to turn, travel ” ) .”

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 سپردن • ( separdan ) ( present stem سپار ( sepâr ) or سپر ( separ ) ) 

 ( archaic ) to traverse , to pass 

 ( archaic ) to tread , to trample

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [script needed] ( abespurdan, abespār- /⁠ʾp̄spwltn', 'p̄sp'l-⁠/ , “ to commit , entrust , consign ” ) , [ 2 ] from Proto-Iranian *upa- + *spar ( “ to hand over, entrust ” ) , which according to Cheung is from Proto-Indo-European *sper- ( “ to deliver, secure ” ) , though the cognates he gives, from Proto-Germanic *sparāną ( “ to be thrifty ” ) , are thought to derive from Proto-Indo-European *speh₁- ( “ to succeed, prosper ” ) instead. [ 3 ]

 [ edit ]

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 سپردن • ( sepordan ) ( present stem سپار ( sepâr ) or سپر ( sepor ) ) 

 to commit 
 1991 , “ یار [ Companion ]” (Pop), performed by Leila Forouhar: اى یار دلتو به هيچكى نسپار 
اونو واسه من نكهدار ey yâr, deleto be hički nasepâr 
uno vâs-e man negahdâr Oh companion, do not commit your heart to anyone
Save that for me 

 to entrust , commend 

 to deposit 

 to order
