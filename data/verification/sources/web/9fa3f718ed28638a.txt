 English
 [ edit ] 

 Mucro of Hypogastrura manubrialis (a species of springtail ) 
 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Latin mucro . From 17th century.

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 mucro ( plural mucros or mucrones ) 

 ( botany , zoology ) A pointed end, often sharp, abruptly terminating an organ , such as a projection at the tip of a leaf; the posterior tip of a cuttlebone ; or the distal part of the furcula in Collembola .

 [ edit ]

 Anagrams
 [ edit ]

 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. Perhaps individualizing n -stem derivative of a lost adjective *mūkro- ( “ pointed, sharp ” ) , from a Proto-Italic *meuk-ro- , *mouk-ro- , derivative of a Proto-Indo-European root *h₂mewḱ- , also seen in Ancient Greek ἀμύσσω ( amússō , “ to tear, rip ” ) , ἀμυκάλαι ( amukálai , “ arrowtips ” ) , and perhaps also in Lithuanian mùšti ( “ strike ” ) . De Vaan considers the PIE etymology to be possibe, though he also suggests that the Latin and Greek terms may derive from an otherwise unknown substrate language. [ 1 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈmʊ.kroː] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈmuː.kro] 

 Short u is indicated in most dictionaries including recent ones (early 21st century). De Vann indicates long ū. [ 1 ] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 mūcrō   m ( genitive mūcrōnis ) ; third declension 

 A sharp point , especially the point of a sword .

 ( figuratively ) A sword.

 A sharp edge .
