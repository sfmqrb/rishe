 This Proto-Indo-European entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Indo-European
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Sometimes compared to *h₁eǵʰ- ( “ hedgehog ” ) despite formal differences.

 This etymology is missing or incomplete. Please add to it, or discuss it at the Etymology scriptorium . 
Particularly: “Possibly a Semitic borrowing. Compare Arabic حَيَّة   f ( ḥayya , “ snake, worm, wyrm ” ) , Aramaic חויא   m ( ḥewyā ) , Classical Syriac ܚܘܝܐ   m ( ḥewyā ) , Egyptian 

   m ( ḥfꜣw, /ˈħafʀaw/ , “ snake ” ) ; as well as the feminine forms found in Aramaic חוויתא ( ḥwāwwīṯā ) and Classical Syriac ܚܘܘܝܬܐ ( ḥwāwwīṯā ) . Possibly from an intermediate masculine term, and suffixed with *-is   f .”

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 *h₁ógʷʰis   f [ 1 ] 

 snake 
 Synonym: *h₂éngʷʰis 

 a mythic serpent or dragon that is slain by a great hero in Indo-European mythology [ 2 ] 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 *h₂éngʷʰis ( “ snake ” ) has replaced this word in most of the Western and Central Indo-European dialects.

 Reconstruction notes
 [ edit ] 

 Beekes reconstructs *h₃égʷʰis because “the absence of reflexes of Brugmann's Law points to IE e -vocalism”. [ 3 ] 

 Alternative reconstructions
 [ edit ] 

 *egʷʰi-s , *ogʷʰi-s , *eǵʰi-s [ 4 ] 

 *h₁égʷʰ-ōy ~ *h₁(e)gʷʰ-y-ós ( hysterokinetic, possibly with *-ōy ) [ 5 ] 

 *h₁ḗgʷʰ-s ~ *h₁égʷʰ-os [ 5 ] 

 *h₃égʷʰi-s [ 6 ] [ 3 ]
