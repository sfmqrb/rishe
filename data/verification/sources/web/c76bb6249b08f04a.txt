 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Disputed. According to Beekes from Proto-Indo-European *kyew- , thus cognate with Sanskrit च्यु ( cyu ) . However, Beekes notes that a pre-form *kyéw-e-to ought to yield **σέεται . [ 1 ] The LIV suggests that the term was analogically remodeling after the s-aorist ἔσσευα ( ésseua ) , [ 2 ] though Beekes argues that a PIE sigmatic aorist *kyéw-s-m̥ should yield **-(σ)σῆα ( **-(s)sêa ) . Another theory suggests that the term was inherited from *kyéw-ye-ti , though Beekes argues that the expected outcome of such a form in Greek would be **σέιομαι ( **séiomai ) . Ultimately, Beekes suggests that the -ευ- ( -eu- ) may have been restored on the model of an original athematic present *kyéw-to . [ 1 ] Frisk and Chantraine, on the other hand, consider it to be related to κινέω ( kinéō ) and κίω ( kíō ) . [ 3 ] [ 4 ]

 [ edit ]

 ( 1 st CE Egyptian ) IPA ( key ) : /ˈse.wo/ 

 ( 4 th CE Koine ) IPA ( key ) : /ˈse.βo/ 

 ( 10 th CE Byzantine ) IPA ( key ) : /ˈse.vo/ 

 ( 15 th CE Constantinopolitan ) IPA ( key ) : /ˈse.vo/ 

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 σεύω • ( seúō ) 

 to chase , drive , impel 

 ( middle voice ) to rush , hasten , hurry
