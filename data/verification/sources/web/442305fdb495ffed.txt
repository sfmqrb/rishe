 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Perhaps from Proto-Indo-European *h₁ergʰ- ( “ to mount ” ) , [ 1 ] whence also perhaps Hittite [script needed] ( arkatta , “ to mount sexually ” ) . [ 2 ] Frisk suggests that term may constitute an iterative-intensive formation from ἔρχομαι ( érkhomai , “ to come, go ” ) , for which see there for more. Watkins posits a connection with ὄρχις ( órkhis , “ testicle ” ) , which appears semantically tenuous. [ 3 ] Alternatively, the Proto-Indo-European is reconstructed as *h₃erǵʰ- ( “ to copulate; ardent ” ) (which appears more plausible based on basic sound correspondences) and is compared with Proto-Germanic *argaz ( “ indecent ” ) . [ 4 ]

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 ὀρχέομαι • ( orkhéomai ) 

 to dance 
 408 BCE – 334 BCE , Antiphanes Comicus , Collected Works 113.1

 c. 200 CE , Athenaeus , Deipnosophistae 4.155b

 c. 200 CE , Athenaeus , Deipnosophistae 14.631d

 p. 276 BC , Euphorion of Chalcis , Poetic Fragments 87

 460 BCE – 430 BCE , Herodotus , Histories 1.66 

 c. 750 BCE – 650 BCE , Hesiod , Theogony 4 

 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Iliad 18.594 

 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Odyssey 8.371 

 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Odyssey 14.465 

 c. 370 BCE , Xenophon , Cyropaedia 1.3.10 

 c. 370 BCE , Xenophon , Cyropaedia 8.4.12 

 c. 360 BCE , Xenophon , Symposium 7.5 

 to represent by dancing or pantomime 
 125 CE – 180 CE , Lucian , The Dance 80 

 ( figuratively ) to leap , bound 
 458 BCE , Aeschylus , The Libation Bearers 166 

 310 BCE – 240 BCE , Callimachus , Hymn to Delos 139

 ( active voice ) to cause to dance

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 According to Beekes, the term primarily means "to mount " in inscriptions.
