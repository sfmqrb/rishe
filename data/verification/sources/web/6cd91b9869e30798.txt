 Ottoman Turkish
 [ edit ] 

 زنجیر 
 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 զինճիր ( zincir ) — Armeno-Turkish 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Persian زنجیر ( zanjir , “ chain ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 زنجیر • ( zincir ) 

 chain , a series of interconnected rings or links 
 Synonym: سلسله ( silsile ) 

 fetter , a chain used to bind the legs of a person 
 Synonym: بوقاغی ( bukağı )

 Persian

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 This etymology is missing or incomplete. Please add to it, or discuss it at the Etymology scriptorium . 
Particularly: “Detailed but informal discussion at https://archive.fo/g7MTJ ( https://languagehat.com/zanjeer/ ) - check, in particular, the 2025 comment by user "Xerîb". The gist is that the Persian term (and its Iranic relatives) were borrowed from a Middle Indic reflex of Sanskrit शृङ्खल ( śṛṅkhala , “ chain, fetter ” ) , with a phonetic shift from initial ś- > z- that Buyaner intended to investigate. This etymology was originally proposed by Boyce to Henning, apparently from informal oral talks, and later accepted by Henning circa 1963. Sources are cited in the links, to be added as inline references in a future update to this etymology box.”

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 زنجیر • ( zanjīr / zanjir ) ( Tajik spelling занҷир ) 

 chain 
 c. 1260s , Jalāl ad-Dīn Mohammad Rūmī , translated by Reynold A. Nicholson , مثنوی معنوی [ Masnavi-ye-Ma'navi ], volume I, verse 3159: یاد دادش جور اخوان و حسد 
 گفت کان زنجیر بود و ما اسد yâd dâd-aš jawr-i-axwân u hasad 
 guft k-ân zanjîr bûd u mâ asad He spoke to him (Joseph) of the injustice and envy of his brethren: Joseph said, “That was (like) a chain , and I was the lion.
