 This Proto-Indo-European entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Indo-European
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Various theories have been posited:

 According to some, *wiHrós is derived from the verb *weyh₁- ( “ to hunt ” ) (cf. Sanskrit वेति ( véti ) , Lithuanian výti etc.), which would render the reconstruction as *wih₁rós and the original meaning of "hunter".

 Also often derived from *wéyHs ( “ force, strength ” ) .

 Perhaps related to *h₂weyd- (whence *h₂wéydr̥ ) via the Kortlandt effect, a proposed sound law whereby *d sometimes becomes *h₁ . This theory nessecitates a reconstruction of *h₂wih₁rós . [ 1 ] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 *wiHrós   m ( non-ablauting ) [ 2 ] 

 man 
 Synonym: *h₂nḗr 

 husband 

 warrior , hero 

 Alternative reconstructions
 [ edit ] 

 *h₂wih₁rós [ 1 ] 

 Reconstruction notes
 [ edit ] 

 Germanic, Celtic, and Italic forms point to a short *i, with loss of the root laryngeal, which is reconstructed on the basis of Sanskrit and Lithuanian (Balto-Slavic acute, retracted to the root vowel by Hirt's law ) forms.
