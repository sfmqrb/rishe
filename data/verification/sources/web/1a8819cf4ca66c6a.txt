 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Germanic *arun ? 
 Old English earon 
 Middle English aren 
 English are 

 From Middle English aren , from Old English ( Anglian ) earun , earon ( “ are ” , plural ) . Possibly reinforced by the Old Norse plural forms in er- , this displaced the alternative forms Old English sind and bēoþ . In the second person singular it displaced archaic art . Further etymology controversial:

 The English forms, as well as the Old Swedish forms in ær- , could reflect Proto-Germanic preterite-present *ōr ~ *arum , from Proto-Indo-European *h₃e-h₃ór-h₂e ( “ I have risen ” , perfect ) . [ 2 ] [ 3 ] 

 Since they are not the expected outcomes of the Proto-Germanic forms of *wesaną ( “ to be ” ) in *iz- , they would have to be irregular alterations. For Seebold this explanation is still preferable as similar variants in other verbs are not uncommon. [ 4 ]

 The pronunciation /aʊɚ/ arising from confusion of "are" and " our " is rare; however, it occasionally occurs due to the fact that the latter can be elided into /ɑɹ/ in quick speech.

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ]

 Particle
 [ edit ] 

 are ( Lontara spelling ᨕᨑᨙ ) 

 ( used in rhetorical questions ) on earth ; possibly ; approximately ; supposedly ; perchance 
 Inai are arenna? What could his name possibly be? 

 Ante are kamma? How could it possibly be? 

 Apa are mi lanagaukang? What will he possibly do? 

 Pissiapa are ? How many times possibly ? 

 Apa are sallang kubalasangi punna buntingi What will I possibly give in return later if he marries? 

 ( postpositional , emphasizing a known fact ) certainly ; surely ; obviously ; of course 
 Tau are jaʼ inakke I am obviously human. 

 Taia arè a tau kuagànga mange-mange He is certainly not the person I went with. 

 Jukuʼ are nuballi You certainly bought fish. 

 Ka ia are ji appalaʼ Because he is the one who certainly asked for it. 

 ( postpositional ) maybe ; perhaps ; approximately ; probably 
 Sibulang are , ruambulang are Maybe a month, maybe two months. 

 Sikayu are ka, rua are ka Approximately one or two (animals). 

 Taenapa kuasseng, akkule are taena are I do not know yet, maybe possible, maybe not.
