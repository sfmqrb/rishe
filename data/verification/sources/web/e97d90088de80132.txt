 Ottoman Turkish
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Classical Persian هُنَر ( hunar , “ art; craft, ability ” ) , from Middle Persian 𐫍𐫇𐫗𐫡 ( hwnr /⁠hunar⁠/ , “ virtue, ability, skill ” ) , ultimately from Old Persian 𐎢𐎺𐎴𐎼 ( ʰuvnara , “ talent, capability ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 هنر • ( hüner ) ( definite accusative هنری ( hüneri ) , plural هنرلر ( hünerler ) ) 

 craft , dexterity , ingenuity , adroitness 
 Synonyms: اوسته‌لق ( ustalık ) , صنعت ( sanʼat, zanʼat ) , معرفت ( maʼrifet ) 
 هنر صاتمق ― hüner satmak ― to pretend to be clever 

 ability , skill , talent , competence 
 Synonyms: صنعت ( sanʼat, zanʼat ) , مهارت ( maharet ) 
 هنر كوسترمك ― hüner göstermek ― to exhibit a skill or talent 

 art 
 Synonyms: صنعت ( sanʼat, zanʼat ) , فن ( fenn )

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( hwnl ) , 𐫍𐫇𐫗𐫡 ( hwnr /⁠hunar⁠/ , “ virtue, ability, skill ” ) , from Old Persian 𐎢𐎺𐎴𐎼 ( ʰuvnara , “ talent, capability ” ) , from Proto-Iranian *hu-nara , from Proto-Indo-European *h₁su- ( “ good ” ) + *h₂nḗr ( “ man; power, force, vital energy ” ) . Cognate with Old Armenian հնար ( hnar , “ means, way ” ) (from Iranian), Avestan 𐬵𐬎𐬥𐬀𐬭𐬀   m ( hunara , “ skill, strength ” ) , Sanskrit सूनर ( sū-nára , “ possessing vital strength, mighty, prosperous, beautiful ” ) and सुन्दर ( sundara , “ beautiful, charming ” ) .

 Compare also formally identical formations in other Indo-European languages: Old Irish so-nirt , so-nairt ( “ strong ” ) , Welsh hy-nerth ( “ mighty ” ) , Ancient Greek εὐ-ήνωρ ( eu-ḗnōr , “ manly ” ) .
