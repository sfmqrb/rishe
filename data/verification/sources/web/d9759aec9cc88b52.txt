 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from an Italic language, [ 1 ] ultimately from Proto-Italic *līðrā ( “ ingot ” ) . [ 2 ] The immediate source for the Greek borrowing could be Sicel *lītrā . [ 3 ] The Italic word also survives in Latin lībra . Compare the morphology of Ancient Greek στατήρ ( statḗr ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 λῑ́τρᾱ • ( lī́trā )  f ( genitive λῑ́τρᾱς ) ; first declension 

 litra , a Sicilian silver coin
 Epicharumus 5

 Sophron 26

 342 BCE – 291 BCE , Diphilus , The Sicilian 1

 316 BCE – 250 BCE , Posidippus Comicus , The Gaul 2

 384 BCE – 322 BCE , Aristotle , Fragments 436

 libra , a unit of weight
 Palatine Anthology 6.214

 200 BCE – 118 BCE , Polybius , Histories 22.26.19

 Palatine Anthology 10.97

 a box for holding libras
 Synonym: λιτροδόκη ( litrodókē )
