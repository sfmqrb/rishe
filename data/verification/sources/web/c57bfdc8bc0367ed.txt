 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Probably from Proto-Italic *siskʷos , from Proto-Indo-European *siskus ( “ dry ” ) , presumably from *sek- ( “ to dry up ” ) . Cognate with Ancient Greek ἰσχνός ( iskhnós ) , Proto-Celtic *siskʷos , Lithuanian sèkti ( “ to lower oneself, sink, dry out ” ) , Sanskrit असश्चुषी ( asaścuṣī , “ not drying up ” ) , though the phonetic details are unclear. [ 1 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈsɪk.kʊs] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈsik.kus] 

 Adjective
 [ edit ] 

 siccus ( feminine sicca , neuter siccum , comparative siccior ) ; first / second-declension adjective 

 dry 
 Synonym: āridus 
 8 CE , Ovidius , Fasti 4.498–499 : quō simul ac vēnit, frēnātōs curribus anguēs
iungit et aequoreās sicca pererrat aquās And as soon as she has arrived there, she harnesses the bridled serpents to [her] chariots, and wanders dry over the ocean waves. 
(See Ceres (mythology) .) 

 sober 

 thirsty
