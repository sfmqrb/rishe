 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. According to Beekes, probably from Pre-Greek . [ 1 ] Compare the theonym Ἥρα ( Hḗra ) and the proper name Ἥρυλλα ( Hḗrulla ) . 

 An original root *ἡρωϝ- ( *hērōw- ) was traditionally hypothesised by comparison with πάτρως ( pátrōs ) , μήτρως ( mḗtrōs ) . Assuming an original meaning “ protector, defender ” , traditionally also linked to Latin servō ( “ to protect, rescue ” ) , Avestan 𐬵𐬀𐬎𐬭𐬎𐬎𐬀𐬌𐬙𐬌 ( ha u ruua i ti , “ to protect, defend ” ) . [ 2 ] However, this is not possible in view the lack of /w/ in Mycenaean Greek 𐀴𐀪𐀮𐀫𐀁 ( ti-ri-se-ro-e ) . [ 3 ]

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 ἥρως • ( hḗrōs )  m ( genitive ἥρωος or ἥρω ) ; third declension 

 ( Epic ) a hero of the Trojan War : any of the major combatants of the Greek or Trojan forces 
 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Iliad 2.110–111 : ὦ φίλοι ἥρωες Δαναοὶ θεράποντες Ἄρηος
Ζεύς με μέγα Κρονίδης ἄτῃ ἐνέδησε βαρείῃ ô phíloi hḗrōes Danaoì therápontes Árēos
Zeús me méga Kronídēs átēi enédēse bareíēi Oh friends, Danaän heroes , servants of Ares,
Zeus, the great son of Cronos, has ensnared me in a severe delusion. 

 a hero or heroine of the ancient Greek religion : a human or demigod whose shrine was celebrated with chthonic rituals organized by local governments
