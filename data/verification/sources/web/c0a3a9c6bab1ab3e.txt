 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Indo-Iranian *Hagʰás ( “ bad; evil ” ) , from Proto-Indo-European *h₂egʰ-ós , from *h₂egʰ- ( “ to be upset; to be afraid ” ) . Cognate with Avestan 𐬀𐬖𐬀 ( aγa , “ evil ” ) , Ancient Greek ἄχος ( ákhos , “ distress; pain ” ) , Old English eġle (whence English ail ).

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Vedic ) IPA ( key ) : /ɐ.ɡʱɐ́/ 

 ( Classical Sanskrit ) IPA ( key ) : /ɐ.ɡʱɐ/ 

 Adjective
 [ edit ] 

 अ॒घ • ( aghá ) stem 

 bad ; evil ; sinful ; dangerous 
 c. 1500 BCE – 1000 BCE , Ṛgveda 1.42.2 : यो नः॑ पूषन्न् अ॒घो वृको॑ दुः॒शेव॑ आ॒दिदे॑शति ।
अप॑ स्म॒ तं प॒थो ज॑हि ॥ yó naḥ pūṣann aghó vṛ́ko duḥśéva ādídeśati.
ápa sma táṃ pathó jahi. Drive, Pūṣan, from our road the wolf, the wicked inauspicious wolf,
Who lies in wait to injure us.

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 अ॒घ • ( aghá ) stem ,  n 

 evil ; mishap ; sin 
 c. 1500 BCE – 1000 BCE , Ṛgveda 2.41.11 : इन्द्र॑श् च मृ॒ळया॑ति नो॒ न नः॑ प॒श्चाद् अ॒घं न॑शत् ।
भ॒द्रं भ॑वाति नः पु॒रः ॥ índraś ca mṛḷáyāti no ná naḥ paścā́d agháṃ naśat.
bhadráṃ bhavāti naḥ puráḥ. Indra be gracious unto us: sin shall not reach us from behind,
And good shall be before us still.
