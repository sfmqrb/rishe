 Ancient Greek

 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 ἴκκος ( íkkos ) ( mentioned only in the Byzantine Etymologicum Magnum ) 

 ἴππος ( íppos ) — Aeolic 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 PIE word 
 *h₁éḱwos 

 From Proto-Hellenic *íkkʷos (compare Mycenaean Greek 𐀂𐀦 ( i-qo ) ), from Proto-Indo-European *h₁éḱwos , from *h₁oh₁ḱu- ( “ swift ” ) . [ 1 ] The expected inherited form is **έπος ( **épos ) , and there is no explanation for why ῐ̔́ππος ( hĭ́ppos ) has ῐ in place of ε , word-initial rough breathing, and double ππ ( pp ) , each of which is of unclear origin; possibly borrowed from another Indo-European language where such sound changes are regular. Cognates include Sanskrit अश्व॑ ( áśva ) , Latin equus , Lithuanian ašva , Gaulish epos , Old Armenian էշ ( ēš , “ donkey ” ) , Icelandic jór , and Old Irish ech .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 ῐ̔́ππος • ( hĭ́ppos )  m or f ( genitive ῐ̔́ππου ) ; second declension ( Epic , Attic , Ionic , Doric , Koine ) 

 a horse , ( feminine ) a mare 
 90 CE – 100 CE , Revelation 6:8 : καὶ εἶδον, καὶ ἰδοὺ ἵππος χλωρός, καὶ ὁ καθήμενος ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ ὄνομα αὐτῷ [ὁ] Θάνατος, καὶ ὁ ἅδης ἠκολούθει μετ’ αὐτοῦ kaì eîdon, kaì idoù híppos khlōrós, kaì ho kathḗmenos epánō autoû ónoma autōî [ho] Thánatos, kaì ho hádēs ēkoloúthei met’ autoû And I looked, and behold a pale horse : and his name that sat on him was Death, and Hell followed with him. (KJV) 

 ( feminine ) cavalry , horsemen 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 When used as a collective noun ("horse, cavalry"), this word is always feminine singular, even with numerals.
 460 BCE – 430 BCE , Herodotus , Histories 7.41 : μετὰ δὲ ἵππος ἄλλη χιλίη ἐκ Περσέων ἀπολελεγμένη metà dè híppos állē khilíē ek Perséōn apolelegménē and after them came another thousand horsemen chosen out from the Persians
