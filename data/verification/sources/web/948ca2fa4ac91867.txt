 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Italian Mónte Càrlo ( literally “ Mount Charles ” ) . Named after Charles III, Prince of Monaco  (1818–1889).

 A ward of Monaco , Europe , famous for its casinos .

"My dear fellow, why do they go to Monte Carlo ?" was the retort, accompanied by a tolerant shrug.

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Internationalism , based on Italian Monte Carlo . The Italian name literally means “Mount Charles”, from monte ( “ mount, mountain ” ) and the personal name Carlo ( “ Charles ” ) . The former Spélugues district was officially named Monte-Carlo on 1 July 1866 in honour of Charles III, Prince of Monaco .

 Parallel international adaptations include French Monte-Carlo , German Monte-Carlo , Polish Monte Carlo , Portuguese Monte Carlo , Slovak Monte Carlo and Spanish Montecarlo ( “ Monte Carlo ” ) .

 Rhymes: -ɑːrlu 

 Hyphenation: Mon‧te Car‧lo 

 Proper noun
 [ edit ] 

 Monte Carlo 

 Monte Carlo ; a district of Monaco , forming part of the principality's continuous urban area and known especially for its casino .
 Vi ruslet gjennom kasinohagene i Monte Carlo om kvelden. ― We strolled through the casino gardens in Monte Carlo in the evening. 
 2026 February 4, Sten Lundbo, Svein Askheim, “Monte Carlo”, in Store norske leksikon ‎ [1] : Monte Carlo er en by og et distrikt i fyrstedømmet Monaco. Monte Carlo is a city and a district in the Principality of Monaco. 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 Monte Carlo is a district within the city-state of Monaco . It is also commonly described as a city or urban district, but it is not the capital of Monaco.
