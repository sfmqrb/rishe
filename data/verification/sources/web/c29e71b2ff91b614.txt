 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Hellenic *gʷápt͏̌ō , from Proto-Indo-European *gʷh₂bʰ-yé-ti , from *gʷeh₂bʰ- ( “ to dive, dive in ” ) . Cognate with Proto-Germanic *kwēbijaną , [ 1 ] Avestan 𐬘𐬀𐬟𐬭𐬀 ( jafra ) , Persian ژرف ( žarf ) , Lurish گںهںر ( gəhər , a lake ) , and Sanskrit ग॒भी॒र ( gabhīrá ) .

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 βᾰ́πτω • ( bắptō ) 

 to dip ; to submerge 
 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Odyssey 9.391–393 : ὡς δ’ ὅτ’ ἀνὴρ χαλκεὺς πέλεκυν μέγαν ἠὲ σκέπαρνον
εἰν ὕδατι ψυχρῷ βάπτῃ μεγάλα ἰάχοντα
φαρμάσσων· τὸ γὰρ αὖτε σιδήρου γε κράτος ἐστίν· hōs d’ hót’ anḕr khalkeùs pélekun mégan ēè sképarnon
ein húdati psukhrōî báptēi megála iákhonta
pharmássōn; tò gàr aûte sidḗrou ge krátos estín; Translation by Samuel Butler As a blacksmith plunges an axe or hatchet into cold water to temper it—for it is this that gives strength to the iron— [ … ] 

 to dye ; to colour 
 375 BCE , Plato , The Republic 429d : Οὐκοῦν οἶσθα, ἦν δ’ ἐγώ, ὅτι οἱ βαφῆς, ἐπειδὰν βουληθῶσι βάψαι ἔρια ὥστ’ εἶναι ἁλουργά, Oukoûn oîstha, ên d’ egṓ, hóti hoi baphês, epeidàn boulēthôsi bápsai éria hṓst’ eînai halourgá, (please add an English translation of this quotation) 

 460 BCE – 430 BCE , Herodotus , Histories 7.67.1 : [ … ] Σαράγγαι δὲ εἵματα μὲν βεβαμμένα ἐνέπρεπον ἔχοντες, [ … ] [ … ] Sarángai dè heímata mèn bebamména enéprepon ékhontes, [ … ] Translation by George Rawlinson The Sarangians had dyed garments which showed brightly, 

 to baptise
