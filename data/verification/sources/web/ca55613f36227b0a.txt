 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 γρῡ́φς ( grū́phs ) 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. Commonly linked to γρῡπός ( grūpós , “ hook-nosed; aquiline ” ) , which is from Proto-Indo-European *ger- ( “ to turn, twist, bend, wind ” ) , related to Old English crumb , crump ( “ bent, crooked ” ) , Old High German krump .

 Beekes and Klein dismiss an Indo-European origin and instead suggest Pre-Greek ; possibly borrowed via Hittite or some other Anatolian medium from a Semitic word related to Akkadian 𒅗𒆕𒁍 ( karūbu ) and Hebrew כרוב ( kerúv ) , in which case a doublet of χερούβ ( kheroúb ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 γρῡ́ψ • ( grū́ps )  m ( genitive γρῡπός ) ; third declension 

 griffin 
 460 BCE – 430 BCE , Herodotus , Histories 3.116 (compare Aeschylus , Prometheus Bound 395 )
