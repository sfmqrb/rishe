 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [script needed] ( dypʾk' /⁠dēbāg⁠/ , “ brocade ” ) , probably a derivative of Proto-Iranian *diHp- ( “ to shine, light up ” ) , due to the sheen imparted by the gold and silver in traditional brocades; [ 1 ] see Sanskrit दीप् ( dīp , “ to blaze, to glow ” ) for more on the root. [ 2 ] Compare also the Iranian borrowings: Old Armenian դիպակ ( dipak ) , Classical Syriac ܕܝܒܓܐ ( dybgʾ /⁠dēybāḡā⁠/ ) , Arabic دِيبَاج ( dībāj ) . Doublet of دواج ( dawāj  / davâj , “ bed-quilt ” ) .
