 Etymology

 Doublet of Europe .

 Audio : ( file ) 

 Proper noun
 [ edit ] 

 The moon Europa 
 Europa 

 ( Greek mythology ) Several characters, most notably a Phoenician princess abducted to Crete by Zeus .

 ( astronomy ) A moon in Jupiter .
 1990 August, Bradford A. Smith, “Voyage of the Century”, in National Geographic , volume 178 , number 2, page 53 : We now know that Europa's surface is the smoothest of any in the solar system. 

 ( astronomy ) 52 Europa , a main belt asteroid ; not to be confused with the Jovian moon.

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Europe highlighted on a globe. 
 Internationalism ; borrowed from Latin Eurōpa , from Ancient Greek Εὐρώπη ( Eurṓpē ) , used in Ancient Greek both as a geographical name and as the name of a mythological figure. The ultimate origin is uncertain and probably Pre-Greek. The geographical and mythological uses may ultimately represent the same name, but a direct historical connection between them has not been established. The traditional interpretation of the mythological name as εὐρύς ( eurús , “ wide, broad ” ) + ὤψ ( ṓps , “ eye, face ” ) is uncertain, as are proposed connections with Semitic words meaning “evening” or “west”.

 Compare Danish Europa , Swedish Europa , German Europa , Dutch Europa and English Europe .

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 Europa on the back of Zeus in the form of a bull, in a fresco from Pompeii. 
 Jupiter's moon Europa in approximate natural color. 
 A three-dimensional shape model of the asteroid 52 Europa. 
 Internationalism ; ultimately from Latin Eurōpa ( “ Europa ” ) , from Ancient Greek Εὐρώπα ( Eurṓpa , “ Europa ” ) , probably from Pre-Greek .

 Beekes (2004) regards the mythological Europa as an old supernatural figure of probably Pre-Greek date and considers her association with Phoenicia secondary. The original meaning of the name is unknown.

 The astronomical names are eponyms of the mythological figure. Simon Marius proposed the name for Jupiter's moon in 1614, following a suggestion by Johannes Kepler to name the Galilean moons after mythological figures associated with Jupiter. The asteroid 52 Europa , discovered by Hermann Goldschmidt on 4 February 1858, was likewise named after her.
