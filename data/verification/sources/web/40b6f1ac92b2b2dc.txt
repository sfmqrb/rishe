 Alternative forms

 ὠθῶ ( ōthô ) 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Possible Greek relations, even if ruled out by Beekes as highly unlikely, are the verbal present form ἔθει ( éthei ) , the participle ἔθων ( éthōn , “ damaging, plaguing; pushing, tossing (?) ” ) and the nouns ἔθρις ( éthris , “ castrated ram ” ) , ἔθειρα ( étheira , “ horses' hair, mane ” ) . The lemma could be an old lengthened grade of one of the underlying roots. Compare ὄθομαι ( óthomai , “ to show consideration for others, fight shy of ” ) , only used with a negative particle, ὄθη ( óthē , “ worry, care, fear, consideration ” ) and νωθρός ( nōthrós , “ sluggish, slothful ” ) .

 Taking in account Avestan 𐬬𐬁𐬛𐬁𐬌𐬌𐬋𐬌𐬝 ( vādāiiōit̰ , “ he may push back ” ) and Sanskrit अवधीत् ( ávadhīt , “ he killed ” ) (root वध् ( vadh , “ to kill, destroy (a man's ability to procreate) ” ) ), as well as Hittite [script needed] ( ḫuett- tta(ri) , “ to draw, pull ” ) , Beekes constructed a root Proto-Indo-European *h₂wedʰh₁- ( “ to push away, slay ” ) , whence an iterative *h₂wodʰh₁-éye-ti . [ 1 ] Mark that also ἔθρις ( éthris ) seems to be cognate to Sanskrit वध्रि ( vádhri , “ castrated ” ) .

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 ὠθέω • ( ōthéō ) 

 ( transitive ) to push 
 c. 800 BCE – 600 BCE , Homer , Odyssey 11.595–597 : ἦ τοι ὁ μὲν σκηριπτόμενος χερσίν τε ποσίν τε
λᾶαν ἄνω ὤθεσκε ποτὶ λόφον· ἀλλ’ ὅτε μέλλοι
ἄκρον ὑπερβαλέειν, [ … ] ê toi ho mèn skēriptómenos khersín te posín te
lâan ánō ṓtheske potì lóphon; all’ hóte mélloi
ákron huperbaléein, [ … ] Translation by Samuel Butler With hands and feet he [Sisyphus] tried to roll it up to the top of the hill, [ … ] (literally, “to push upwards”)
