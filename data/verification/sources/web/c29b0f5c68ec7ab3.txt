 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Italic *tremō , from Proto-Indo-European *trémti , from *trem- ( “ tremble ” ) . Cognate with Ancient Greek τρέμω ( trémō ) . [ 1 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈtrɛ.moː] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈtrɛː.mo] 

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 tremō ( present infinitive tremere , perfect active tremuī ) ; third conjugation , no supine stem 

 to tremble , shake , shudder at
 Synonym: horreō 
 161 BCE , Publius Terentius Afer , Eunuchus 83–84 : totus, Parmeno, tremo horreoque, postquam aspexi hanc. 2001 translation by John Barsby I’m trembling and shivering all over, Parmeno, at the sight of her. 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 The Carmen Saliare attests to the term tremonti , which is perhaps an archaic third-person plural form. [ 1 ] This word may then faithfully preserve the final *-i in the PIE third-person plural ending *-nti , which was otherwise apocopated in Italic. [ 2 ]
