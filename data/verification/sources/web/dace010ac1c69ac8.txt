 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian BNPŠE , 𐭡𐭭𐭯𐭱𐭤 ( xwad ) , from Old Persian *hvatah . Related to خدا ( xudā  / xodâ , “ God ” ) .

 خود • ( xwad / xod ) 

 ( reflexive pronoun ) self , -self 
 Synonym: خویش ( xwēš  / xiš ) ( more literary ) 
 c. 1260 , Rumi , The Masnawī : حمله بر خود می‌کنی ای ساده مرد، همچو آن شیری که بر خود حمله کرد. hamla bar xud mē-kunī ay sāda mard, hamču ān šērī ki bar xud hamla kard. You are attacking yourself , o simple man, like that lion who attacked himself . 

 2009 , Shadmehr Aghili, “ تقدیر ( taqdir ) ”: تو با خودت هم دشمنی. to bâ xod -et ham došman-i. You are an enemy even with your self . 

 ( emphatic ) one's own ( in possessive constructions ) 
 Synonym: خویش ( xwēš  / xiš ) ( more literary ) 
 مادر خود را نمی‌شناسی؟ ( formal Persian ) mādar-i xwad rā namē-šināsī?  / mâdar-e xod râ nemi-šenâsi? Don’t you recognize your own mother? 

 مادر خودتو نمی‌شناسی؟ ( colloquial Tehrani ) mâdar-e xodeto nemi-šnâsi? 

 مادر خو ره نمی‌شناسی؟ ( colloquial Kabuli ) mādar xu ra namē-šnāsī? 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 خود ( xwad  / xod ) can occur both in isolation and before a suffixed pronominal clitic such as ـم ( -am , “ my ” ) , ـت ( -at, et , “ your ” ) , and ـش ( -aš, -eš , “ his/her ” ) . In isolation, it serves as a general reflexive pronoun for any subject:

 خود را دیدم xod-râ didam I saw [my]self 

 خود را دیدی xod-râ didi you saw [your]self 

 The pronominal clitics specify the pronoun:

 خودم را دیدم xod-am-râ didam I saw myself 

 خودت را دیدی xod-at-râ didi you saw yourself 

 Colloquial usage differs slightly in Iran and Afghanistan

 In colloquial Iranian Persian, the suffixed forms are almost always used, and the isolated forms are more literary.

 In colloquial Dari Persian, the isolated forms are forms are significantly more common, but خود ( xwad  / xod ) tends to reduce to خو ( xwa  / xo ) , and ezafe is not needed after a noun.
 Thus colloquial Dari: مادر خو ― mādar xu ― your [own] mother ; Where, in formal Dari (and formal Iranian Persian), the absence of ezafe would be incorrect.

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian xwd ( xōd , “ helm ” ) , borrowed from Parthian xwwd ( *xōδ , “ helmet ” ) , from Old Iranian , ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *kadʰ- ( “ to guard, cover, care for, protect ” ) . The Parthian word is cognate with Old Persian 𐎧𐎢𐎭𐎠 ( xaudā- , “ hat ” ) , Avestan 𐬑𐬀𐬊𐬜𐬀 ( xaoδa , “ helmet ” ) , Ossetian худ ( xud , “ hat ” ) , English hat , hood , etc. The inherited form of the Persian is خوی ( xoy ) . Akin to Old Armenian խոյր ( xoyr ) , արտախոյր ( artaxoyr ) , Georgian ხვირი ( xviri ) , Arabic خُوذَة ( ḵūḏa ) , Iranian borrowings.
