 This Proto-Indo-European entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Indo-European
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain.

 If the term is reconstructed as *h₁órǵʰis , the term may derive from a root *h₁erǵʰ- , [ 1 ] perhaps whence also Ancient Greek ὀρχέομαι ( orkhéomai ) .

 If the term is reconstructed as *h₃órǵʰis or *h₃érǵʰis , it may derive from a root *h₃erǵʰ- , perhaps whence also Proto-Germanic *argaz .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 *h₁órǵʰis [ 2 ] 

 testicle 

 Reconstruction notes
 [ edit ] 

 Kloekhorst prefers to reconstruct the term with an initial *h₃ laryngeal as numerous descendants in daughter languages appear to have been affected by the o-coloring of vowels, such as ὄρχις ( órkhis ) . Beekes concurs and suggests that the majority of descendants show o-vocalism. According to Kloekhorst, the reconstruction with the initial *h₁ laryngeal is exclusively based on Lithuanian er̃žilas , which Kloekhorst argues is more likely the result of "Rozwadowski’s change," a dialectal sound change in which word-initial e- and a- were confused.

 Kloekhorst suggests that the Hittite and Greek terms may reflect a zero-grade form *h₃ṛg-i- , [ 3 ] which he suggests is the more probable reconstruction as—according to Kloekhorst— Avestan ərəzī and Old Armenian որձիք ( orjikʻ ) are also zero-grade formations. Though, Beekes suggests that an o-grade pre-form is possible for the Armenian term. [ 4 ] Likewise, Martirosyan argues that the Armenian term likely reflects an o-grade formation, citing Ancient Greek ὄρχις ( órkhis ) and Middle Irish uirgge as parallels.

 The Albanian term herdhe may derive from an e-grade form *h₃erǵʰi- , [ 5 ] though Matasović argues that the initial h- could be a secondary development. Beekes suggests that the Ancient Greek term could also be explained from an e-grade formation. [ 4 ] Similarly, the linguist Craig Melchert has argued that the Hittite term may be derived from *h₃erǵʰis provided that the initial laryngeal during the transition to Hittite. Kortlandt rejects this hypothesis, and prefers to derive the Hittite term from an o-grade pre-form. [ 6 ] 

 The linguist Eric P. Hamp prefers to reconstruct the term with an initial *h₄ laryngeal, [ 7 ] a proposed Proto-Indo-European phoneme whose existence is disputed. [ 8 ] Regardless, according to Hamp, such a reconstruction would explain the provenance of the Albanian term. [ 7 ]
