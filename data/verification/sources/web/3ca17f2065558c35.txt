 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Learned borrowing from Sanskrit हस्त ( hasta ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Standard Hindi ) IPA ( key ) : /ɦəst̪/ , [ɦɐst̪] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 हस्त • ( hast )  m 

 hand 
 Synonyms: see Thesaurus: हाथ 
 कलाकार के हस्त की निपुणता चित्र में साफ़ दिखाई देती है। kalākār ke hast kī nipuṇtā citra mẽ sāf dikhāī detī hai. The skill of the artist's hand is clearly visible in the painting.

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *ǵʰes- der. 
 Proto-Indo-Iranian *ȷ́ʰástas 
 Sanskrit हस्त 

 Inherited from Proto-Indo-Iranian *ȷ́ʰástas , from Proto-Indo-European *ǵʰes- .

 Cognate with Old Persian 𐎭𐎿𐎫 ( d-s-t /⁠dasta⁠/ ) (whence modern Persian دست ( dast ) ), Avestan 𐬰𐬀𐬯𐬙𐬀 ( zasta , “ hand ” ) , Ancient Greek χείρ ( kheír , “ hand ” ) , Latin hir ( “ hand ” ) . [ 1 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Vedic ) IPA ( key ) : /ɦɐ́s.tɐ/ 

 ( Classical Sanskrit ) IPA ( key ) : /ɦɐs̪.t̪ɐ/ 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 हस्त॑ • ( hásta ) stem ,  m 

 the hand 
 c. 1500 BCE – 1000 BCE , Ṛgveda 2.33.7 : क्व१॒॑ स्य ते॑ रुद्र मृळ॒याकु॒र् हस्तो॒ यो अस्ति॑ भेष॒जो जला॑षः ।
अ॒प॒भ॒र्ता रप॑सो॒ दैव्य॑स्या॒भी नु मा॑ वृषभ चक्षमीथाः ॥ kvà syá te rudra mṛḷayā́kur hásto yó ásti bheṣajó jálāṣaḥ.
apabhartā́ rápaso dáivyasyābhī́ nú mā vṛṣabha cakṣamīthāḥ. Where is that gracious hand of yours, O Rudra, that gives health and brings comfort,
O remover of the woe that Gods have sent us? O Strong One, look on me with compassion. 

 c. 400 BCE , Mahābhārata Adi Parva.165.46: हस्ते गृहीत्वा तद् रक्षो दूरम् अन्यत्र नीतवान् ।
पुच्छे गृहीत्वा तुण्डेन गरुडः पन्नगं यथा ॥ haste gṛhītvā tad rakṣo dūram anyatra nītavān.
pucche gṛhītvā tuṇḍena garuḍaḥ pannagaṃ yathā. Grabbing the demon in [his] hand , [Bhima] dragged him over a great distance
Like an eagle [drags] a serpent, having grabbed it by the tail with its beak. 

 an elephant's trunk 

 the forearm (a measure of length from the elbow to the tip of the middle finger)

 ( astronomy ) the 11th (13th) lunar asterism (represented by a hand and containing five stars, identified by some with part of the constellation Corvus )

 ( prosody ) an anapest
