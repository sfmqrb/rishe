 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [script needed] ( kaž, gaz ) , from Proto-Indo-European *kog- , *keg- , *keng- ( “ peg, hook, claw ” ) . Related to چنگ ( čang ) . Cognate with English hook .

 Adjective
 [ edit ] 

 کژ • ( kaž ) ( comparative کژتَر , superlative کژتَرین ) 

 alternative form of کج ( kaj , “ crooked ” ) 
 c. 1260s , Jalāl ad-Dīn Mohammad Rūmī , translated by Reynold A. Nicholson , مثنوی معنوی [ Masnavi-ye-Ma'navi ], volume II, verse 317: و آن فسون دیو در دلهای کژ 
میرود چون کفش کژ در پای کژ u ān fasūn-i dēw dar dil-hā-i kaž 
mē-rawad čōn kafš-i kaž dar pā-i kaž While the spell of the Devil goes into crooked hearts
as a crooked shoe on to a crooked foot.

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [script needed] ( kač ) . Akin to Old Armenian կաճ ( kač , “ felt ” ) , Old Georgian ყაჭი ( q̇ač̣i ) , Proto-Nakh *q̇aač̣ , Aramaic קָזָא / קָאזָא ( qāzzā ) , Arabic خَزّ ( ḵazz ) , قَزّ ( qazz , “ silk ” ) , possibly also Sanskrit कोशज ( kośaja , “ coming from the cocoon, silk ” ) and Ancient Greek χάσδιον ( khásdion , “ a kind of silk-cloth ” ) .
