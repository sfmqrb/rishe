 شَاخَ • ( šāḵa ) I ( non-past يَشِيخُ ( yašīḵu ) , verbal noun شَيَخ ( šayaḵ ) or شُيُوخَة ( šuyūḵa ) or شُيُوخِيَّة ( šuyūḵiyya ) or شَيْخُوخَة ( šayḵūḵa ) or شَيْخُوخِيَّة ( šayḵūḵiyya ) ) 

 ( intransitive ) to become elderly ; to age ; to senesce 
 2019 , Cairokee , “ مَا عَاد صَغِيرًا [ mā ʕād ṣaḡīran , No Longer A Child ]”, in أبناء البطة السوداء [ The Ugly Ducklings ]: قد شاخ صغيرك يا أمي / ما عاد صغيرًا يا أمي Your little one has grown old , Mother / He is no longer little, Mother

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *ḱókh₂eh₂ 
 Proto-Indo-Iranian *ćā́kʰaH 
 Persian شاخ 

 Inherited from Proto-Indo-Iranian *ćā́kʰaH , from Proto-Indo-European *ḱókh₂eh₂ ( “ branch ” ) . Cognate with Sanskrit शाखा ( śākhā , “ branch, twig, limb, finger ” ) , Old Armenian ցախ ( cʻax , “ branch, twig ” ) , Lithuanian šakà ( “ branch ” ) , Gothic 𐌷𐍉𐌷𐌰 ( hōha , “ plough ” ) , Old East Slavic соха ( soxa , “ stake, club, brace, plough ” ) , Old Irish géc ( “ branch ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 Dari

 Iranian Persian

 Tajik

 шох 

 شاخ • ( šāx / šâx ) ( Tajik spelling шох ) 

 horn , antler 

 a branch , bough , shoot , sucker , twig 

 the arm , from the tip of the finger to the shoulder

 the leg , from the groin to the foot

 a small cut from a main stream

 the spur of a cock 

 a rib 

 a bit , piece 

 a gusset , gore 

 the forehead 

 the main beam in the roof of a house

 a cup of wine (from a custom prevailing in Jurjistān, to drink wine out of horns), a goblet , a drinking-horn

 wine mixed with rose-water

 a horn wherein they keep civet , a scent-box

 civet 

 the civet-cat

 spices , perfumes
