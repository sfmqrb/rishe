 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Italic *flōs 
 Latin flōs 

 A root noun interpreted as an s -stem noun, from Proto-Italic *flōs , from Proto-Indo-European *bʰleh₃-s ( “ flower, blossom ” ) , from *bʰleh₃- ( “ to bloom ” ) . [ 1 ] Cognates include Ancient Greek φύλλον ( phúllon ) , Gothic 𐌱𐌻𐍉𐌼𐌰 ( blōma ) and Old English blōstm , blæd ( “ leaf ” ) ( English blossom , blade ).

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈfɫoːs] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈflɔs] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 flōs   m ( genitive flōris ) ; third declension 

 flower , blossom 
 8 CE , Ovidius , Fasti 3.253–254 : ferte deae flōrēs : gaudet flōrentibus herbīs haec dea Bring flowers for the goddess; this goddess takes pleasure in flowering plants. 

 c. 37 BCE – 30 BCE , Vergilius , Georgicon 4.109–111 : Invitent croceis [apes] halantes floribus horti
Et custos furum atque avium cum falce saligna
Hellespontiaci servet tutela Priapi. May gardens bright, fragrant with flower , lure them [the bees] and Hellespontian Priap with his willow scythe the robbing bee and the birds keep away. 

 ( figuratively ) the best kind or part of something

 ( figuratively ) the prime ; best state of things

 ( figuratively ) an ornament or embellishment
