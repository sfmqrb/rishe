 Etymology

 Adjective
 [ edit ] 

 परुष • ( paruṣ ) ( indeclinable , Urdu spelling پرش )

 uneven

 cruel

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *pelH- der. 
 Proto-Indo-European *pelHusós 
 Proto-Indo-Iranian *parHušás 
 Sanskrit परुष 

 Inherited from Proto-Indo-Iranian *parHušás , from Proto-Indo-European *pelHu-só-s , from the root *pelH- ( “ gray, pale ” ) , related to पलित ( palitá , “ gray, hoary ” ) . Cognate with Avestan 𐬞𐬊𐬎𐬭𐬎𐬱𐬀 ( po u ruša , “ gray (of hair) ” ) , Persian پیر ( pir , “ old ” ) , Latin pallidus , Ancient Greek πελιτνός ( pelitnós ) , Old Armenian ալիք ( alikʻ , “ wave, gray hair ” ) .

 Adjective
 [ edit ] 

 प॒रु॒ष • ( paruṣá ) stem 

 spotted , variegated , dirty - colored 
 c. 1500 BCE – 1000 BCE , Ṛgveda 5.27.5 : यस्य मा परुषाः शतमुद्धर्षयन्त्युक्षणः। yasya mā paruṣāḥ śatamuddharṣayantyukṣaṇaḥ. From whom a hundred oxen, all of speckled hue , delight my heart... 

 knotty , rough , hard , stiff , rugged , rough , uneven , shaggy 
 c. 1200 BCE – 1000 BCE , Atharvaveda 

 intertwined with creepers (as a tree )

 piercing , keen , sharp , violent , harsh , severe , unkind

 an arrow
