 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian brʾcytn' ( brāzidan , “ to shine, gleam ” ) , from Proto-Indo-European *bʰreh₁ǵ- ( “ to gleam, shine ” ) . 

 Cognates

 Cognate with Polish brzeżdżyć ( “ to dawn ” ) , Russian бре́зжить ( brézžitʹ , “ to dawn ” ) , Lithuanian brėkšti ( “ to dawn ” ) , Albanian bardhë ( “ white ” ) , Danish bjært ( “ bright ” ) , English bright , Faroese and Icelandic bjartur ( “ bright ” ) , Gothic 𐌱𐌰𐌹𐍂𐌷𐍄𐍃 ( bairhts , “ bright, clear; evident ” ) , Norwegian Nynorsk bjart ( “ bright ” ) , Scots bricht ( “ bright ” ) , Swedish bjärt ( “ bright ” ) , Welsh berth ( “ beautiful, fair, fine ” ) .
