 English
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 African bush elephant ( Loxodonta africana ) 
 Asian elephant ( Elephas maximus ) 
 From Middle English elefant , elefaunt , from Old French elefant , elefan , olifant , re-latinized in Middle French as elephant , from Latin elephantus , from Ancient Greek ἐλέφᾱς ( eléphās ) (gen. ἐλέφαντος ( eléphantos ) ). Believed to be derived from an Afroasiatic form such as Proto-Berber *eḷu ( “ elephant ” ) (compare Tamahaq êlu , Tamasheq alu ) or Egyptian ꜣbw ( “ elephant; ivory ” ) . More at ivory . Replaced Middle English olifant (from the aforementioned Old French form, from Vulgar Latin *olifantus ), which replaced Old English elpend ( “ elephant ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 elephant ( countable and uncountable , plural elephants ) 

 A large mammal of the family Elephantidae in the order Proboscidea , having a trunk , and native to Africa and Asia .
 Synonym: elephantid

To All the Nobilitee of this Flourishyng Realme of England, Thomas Elyote Knight Desyreth Increase of Vertue and Honour. ”, in [ Aelius Lampridius ], translated by Thomas Elyote, The Image of Gouernaunce Complied of the Actes and Sentences Notable, of the Most Noble Emperour Alexander Seuerus ,   [ … ] , [ London ] : [ … ] Thomas Berthelett , published 1549 , →OCLC : [A] puiſſant olyphant being brought to the place, the grehound ſeming to reioice at the greatnes of the beaſt, rouſed him, and after two or three queſtynges, he lept to the great Olyphante , and after a longe fight ouerthrew hym and kylled hym. So I doubt not but that ſome men [ … ] whiche beeyng ones called to aucthoritee, ioygned with libertee, will inforce theim ſelfes to make theyr miniſtracions noble and excellent. 

 ( figurative ) Anything huge and ponderous .

 ( paper , printing ) Synonym of elephant paper .

 ( UK , childish ) used when counting to add length, so that each count takes about one second
