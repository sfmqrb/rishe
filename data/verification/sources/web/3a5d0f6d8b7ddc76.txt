 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 رَانَ • ( rāna ) I ( non-past يَرِينُ ( yarīnu ) , verbal noun رَيْن ( rayn ) ) 

 to take possession of [ with عَلَى ( ʕalā ) or بِ ( bi ) or accusative ‘something’ ]

 to seize , to overcome [ with عَلَى ( ʕalā ) or بِ ( bi ) or accusative ‘someone’ ] ( of passion or other intense emotion ) 

 to prevail [ with عَلَى ( ʕalā ) ‘somewhere’ ] ( e.g. of silence )

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [script needed] ( lʾn' /⁠rān⁠/ , “ thigh ” ) , from Proto-Iranian *hrā́nah , from Proto-Indo-Iranian *srā́nas , from Proto-Indo-European *srḗn-s .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 Dari

 ران 

 Iranian Persian

 Tajik

 рон 

 ران • ( rân ) ( plural ران‌ها ( rân-hâ ) ) 

 thigh 
 c. 1011 , Abu'l-Qāsim Firdawsī , “The Tale of Rustam and Isfandiyār”, in شاهنامه [ Book of Kings ]‎ [1] : ستبرست بازوت چون ران شیر
برو یال چون اژدهای دلیر sitabr ast bāzū-t čūn rān -e šēr
bar u yāl čūn aždahā-yi dalēr Your upper arm is stout like the lion's thigh ,
Your chest and mane like a valiant dragon. (Classical Persian romanization)
