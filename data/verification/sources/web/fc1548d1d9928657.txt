 Etymology

 From vir ( “ man, male ” ) + -tūs ( abstract noun-forming suffix ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈwɪr.tuːs] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈvir.tus] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 virtūs   f ( genitive virtūtis ) ; third declension 

 manhood , manliness , virility 

 courage , valor , resoluteness , gallantry 
 Synonyms: spīritus , fortitūdō , fīdūcia , animus 
 c. 52 BCE , Julius Caesar , Commentarii de bello Gallico 1.1 : Quā dē causā Helvētiī quoque reliquōs Gallōs virtūte praecēdunt, quod ferē cotīdiānīs proeliīs cum Germānīs contendunt, cum aut suīs fīnibus eōs prohibent aut ipsī in eōrum fīnibus bellum gerunt. Translation by W. A. McDevitte and W. S. Bohn For which reason the Helvetii also surpass the rest of the Gauls in valor , as they contend with the Germans in almost daily battles, when they either repel them from their own territories, or themselves wage war on their frontiers. 

 virtue , goodness 
 Synonyms: bonum , rēctum 
 Antonyms: vitium , culpa , malum , iniūria , noxa , peccātum , dēlīctum , error 

 excellence , merit , worth , character 

 ( figuratively ) strength ( of medicine or herbs )
