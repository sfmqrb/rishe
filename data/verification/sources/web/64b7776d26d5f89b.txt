 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *Heh₃l- der. 
 Proto-Italic *līmos ? 
 Latin līmus 

 Possibly from Proto-Italic *līmos ( “ oblique ” ) , with no known cognates outside of Italic. [ 1 ] Possibly from Proto-Indo-European *Heh₃l- ( “ to bend ” ) . [ 2 ] Compare perhaps Proto-Germanic *limuz ( “ limb, branch ” ) .

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 Somewhat uncertain; maybe from Proto-Indo-European *h₂leyH- ( “ to smear ” ) or *(s)ley-mo- ( “ slime ” ) . Possible cognates include λίμνη ( límnē , “ marsh, pool, lake ” ) , Sanskrit लिनाति ( lināti , “ sticks, stays, adheres to; slips into, disappears ” ) , Ukrainian слимак ( slymak , “ snail ” ) , Old Church Slavonic слина ( slina , “ spittle ” ) , Old Irish sligim ( “ to smear ” ) , leinam ( “ to follow ” , literally “ to stick to ” ) , Irish lean , Welsh llyfn ( “ smooth ” ) , English slime . According to De Vaan, Ancient Greek λεῖμαξ ( leîmax , “ snail ” ) is probably conversely borrowed from Latin. [ 1 ]
