 This Proto-Indo-European entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Indo-European
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Seemingly from an otherwise unattested root *steh₃- suffix with *-mn̥ . [ 1 ] Another possible derivative of the underlying root is present in στωμύλος ( stōmúlos , “ wordy, loquacious ” ) , though—according to the etymology advanced by Vine—it would actually implying a root *stem- , which is incompatible with the reconstruction proffered by Kloekhorst. [ 2 ]

 Reconstruction notes
 [ edit ] 

 According to Kloekhorst, the Luwian term requires a pre-form of the shape *stHm-en- , and the Greek evidence confirms that the laryngeal was *h₃ . [ 1 ] Kroonen, however, argues that the Germanic evidence for e-grade vocalism renders the reconstruction *stéh₃mn̥ ~ *sth₃méns impossible. [ 5 ] The short-vowel in Avestan 𐬯𐬙𐬀𐬨𐬀𐬥 ( staman ) is unexpected; a pre-form *stéh₃mn̥ should have yielded **𐬯𐬙𐬁𐬨𐬀𐬥 ( **stāman ) . However, according to Lubotsky, vowels in Avestan are often unreliable, and unetymological short vowels sometimes appear before clitics. Given that in both attestations of 𐬯𐬙𐬀𐬨𐬀𐬥 ( staman ) the term appears before clitics, it is possible that the short vowel is secondary. [ 6 ] 

 As an alternative to the reconstruction *stéh₃mn̥ , Melchert posits a reconstruction *stómh₁-mn̥ , which could have undergone laryngeal loss via the Saussure-Hirt effect, yielding *stómmn̥ , which itself would have undergone degemination, producing *stómn̥ . According to this theory, the Hittite term actually derives from *stomh₁-mén- , itself an internal derivative of *stómh₁-mn̥ . This theory does require the reconstruction of an otherwise rare type of acrostatic *mn̥ -stem, though—as justification for this reconstruction—Melchert compares πῶμα ( pôma ) . [ 2 ]
