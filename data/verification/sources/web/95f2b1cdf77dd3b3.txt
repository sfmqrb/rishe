 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( kwtk' /⁠kōdak⁠/ ) , 𐫞𐫇𐫅𐫐 ( qwdk /⁠kōdak⁠/ , “ young, small; baby ” ) (whence Southern Luri کوئک ( köak , “ child ” ) ), from Proto-Iranian *kawta-ka- , from Proto-Iranian *kawta- ( “ small, diminutive ” ) , tentatively from Proto-Indo-European *(s)kaw- ( “ small, diminutive (affectionately) ” ) . [ 1 ] Cognate with Avestan 𐬐𐬎𐬙𐬀𐬐𐬀- ( kutaka- , “ small ” ) , and possibly Sanskrit कुमार ( kumāra , “ boy, prince ” ) ; see the latter for more possible cognates. Compare the Iranian borrowings: Old Armenian կոտակ ( kotak , “ little, short ” ) , կոտիկ ( kotik , “ baby animal ” ) , Armenian կոտիկ ( kotik ) , կուտիկ ( kutik , “ puppy ” ) , Chagatai قودوق ( qoduq , “ donkey foal ” ) , Arabic كوتي ( kūtī , “ short person ” ) , possibly also Hungarian kutya , Bulgarian ку́че ( kúče , “ dog ” ) . Doublet of کوتاه and کوچک . [ 2 ]
