 Arabic
 [ edit ] 

 هِرّ 
 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Compare هَدَرَ ( hadara , “ to roar ” ) .

 It has been suggested that, in the senses “purr” and “cat”, the word could be onomatopoeic, and/or that it could be related to various other Afro-Asiatic words, such as Classical Syriac ܗܪܪܬܐ ( harrārtā , “ cat ” ) , Gafat [script needed] ( urrä , “ cat ” ) , Somali (h)urri ( “ cat ” ) , and possibly Akkadian 𒊓𒀀 ( šurānum ) , the source of Arabic سِنَّوْر ( sinnawr ) (see references in that entry for more). [ 1 ]

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 هَرَّ • ( harra ) I ( non-past يَهِرُّ ( yahirru ) , verbal noun هَرِير ( harīr ) or هَرّ ( harr ) ) 

 ( ambitransitive ) to purr , to whirr , to bray , to grumble , to snarl , to howl 

 ( transitive ) to abhor , to detest , to loathe
