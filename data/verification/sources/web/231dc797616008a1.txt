 English Jack 

 From Middle English Jak , Jakke (also Jackin , Jankyn , etc.), from Anglo-Norman Jakke , *Janke , from Middle Dutch Janke , diminutive of Jan ( “ John ” ) ; equivalent to John +‎ -kin . Doublet of Yankee .

 It can be also used as nickname for Jacob . Though the name was originally a pet form, it has become more of an independent name. See also Middle French Hennequin , Jannequin , Middle Dutch Janneken , English Jake and Jacques .

 Proper noun
 [ edit ] 

 Jack ( countable and uncountable , plural Jacks ) 

 A unisex given name derived from a pet form of the name John . Occasionally a diminutive of other given names such as Jackson , Jacob , Jacqueline or Jonathan .

there's many a gentle person made a Jack .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 Jack ( plural Jacks ) 

 ( informal ) A placeholder or conventional name for any man, particularly a younger, lower-class man. 
 1946 April 22, Bobby Troup , “ (Get Your Kicks on) Route 66 ”, in New Juke Box Hits , performed by Chuck Berry , published March 1961, side 2, track 2: Well, if you ever plan to motor west / Jack , take my way, it's the highway that's the best / Get your kicks on Route 66 

 ( informal , archaic ) Ellipsis of Jack Tar , a sailor .
 1899 , Joseph Conrad , Heart of Darkness , section 1 : When he went home on leave he rioted on a large scale—pompously. Jack ashore—with a difference—in externals only. 

 ( informal , archaic ) Ellipsis of Jack Rum , a soldier .

 A jacqueminot rose .

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 Despite being a common noun, the word is still treated as a name and capitalized as such. The name is most often used with a descriptive " surname ", showing the type of lad intended.
