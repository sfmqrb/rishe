 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Traditionally derived from Proto-Indo-European *skend- ( “ to jump ” ) , and compared with Latin scandō ( “ to ascend ” ) , Sanskrit स्कन्दति ( skandati , “ to jump, hop, hurry ” ) . The original sense, per Beekes, was probably "a piece of wood hanging on ropes, used by acrobats as well as trap-setters"; under the Indo-European theory, the term would thus be interpreted as "wood for jumping or climbing". Beekes is unconvinced by the semantics, as well as the unexplained -α- ( -a- ) in the Greek form, and prefers to take it as Pre-Greek , [ 1 ] though he admits the possibility of a loan from some other lost Indo-European language, perhaps from the root *skend- mentioned above. [ 2 ]

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 σκᾰ́νδᾰλον • ( skắndălon )  n ( genitive σκᾰνδᾰ́λου ) ; second declension 

 trap , snare 

 enticement , temptation 

 offense , scandal 

 stumbling block
