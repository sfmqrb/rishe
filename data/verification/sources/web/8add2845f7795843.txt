 Arabic
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 In the sense of a knot from Persian تو ( tô , “ twist ” ) . In the sense of having been just a borrowing from Egyptian Arabic تَوّ ( taww ) in its meaning, also Hassaniya Arabic تو ( tow , “ instant ” ) , Marazīq Tunisian Arabic تاو ( tâu , “ moment ” ) , in view of Sabaean 𐩻𐩥 ( ṯw , “ until ” ) borrowed from Old South Arabian , in its more original meaning present in Andalusian Arabic ثَو ذَا ( ṯaw ḏā ) or تَو ذَا ( taw ḏā , “ until now ” ) and Yemeni Arabic تَو ( taw , “ in front of ” ) , and following this prepositional character still most notorious with personal suffixes. Possibly corresponding to سَوْفَ ( sawfa ) and Ge'ez ሶበ ( sobä ) , ሶቤ ( sobe , “ when ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 تَوّ • ( taww )  m ( plural أَتْوَاء ( ʔatwāʔ ) ) 

 ( obsolete ) a knot , a twist or contortion in a rope ; a rope that is twisted of a single strand 

 instantaneousness , immediacy , onlyness , the condition of having been present just i.e. recently and/or alone 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 Though parsing as a noun, the sense of an “instance” is of restricted use, often in the adverbial accusative تَوًّا ( tawwan , “ just; in a bit ” ) , and in prepositional phrases فِي التَوِّ ( fī t-tawwi ) and لِلتَّوِّ ( li-t-tawwi ) which latter in particular also takes personal suffixes instead of the article, for the findability of which constructions this dictionary entry renders the following table:

 Old Anatolian Turkish
 [ edit ] 

 تو 

 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 توك ( tük ) , توی ( tüy ) , دو ( dü ) 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Proto-Turkic *tǖk . Compare Karakhanid تُو ( tǖ ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 IPA ( key ) : /ty(ː)/ 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 تو • ( tü ) ( definite accusative تویی ( tüyi ) , plural تولر ( tüler ) ) 

 hair , a pigmented filament of keratin which grows from a follicle on the skin of humans and other mammals .
 Synonym: صاچ ( saç ) 

 ( strictly ) body hair , the hair of the body as opposed to facial hair and headhair 

 feather , a branching, hair-like structure that grows on the bodies of birds, used for flight, swimming, protection and display.
 Synonym: یوك ( yüg )

 Persian

 From Middle Persian 𐭫𐭪 ( LK /⁠tō⁠/ , “ you, thou ” ) , from Old Persian (compare 𐎬𐎺𐎶 ( tuvam ) ), from Proto-Iranian *tuHám (compare Northern Kurdish tu , Pashto ته ( tə ) , Avestan 𐬙𐬏𐬨 ( tūm ) ), from Proto-Indo-Iranian *tuHám , from Proto-Indo-European *túh₂ .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Standard Urdu ) IPA ( key ) : /t̪uː/ 

 Rhymes: -uː 

 Pronoun
 [ edit ] 

 تُو • ( tū ) ( Hindi spelling तू ) 

 second-person singular subjective personal pronoun : you , thou ; Early Modern English thou 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 تُو ( tū ) is the least formal way of expressing "you" in Urdu and is usually used when the subject is of lower social status (generally in terms of a relationship, not class or caste ) or when the relationship between the speaker and subject is intimate enough to justify informality. This is because of the T-V distinction . Usage of this term varies from region to region. In regions such as Maharashtra , this term is very common and normally used inoffensively by adults for children, as well as among social equals such as siblings, friends, and spouses. However, in the Hindi–Urdu Belt , as well as in Pakistan, تُم ( tum ) is heavily preferred for the aforementioned uses, with تُو ( tū ) chiefly used insultingly. In all regions, تُو ( tū ) is generally the most common pronoun used in abusive or vulgar contexts. When inoffensively addressing an elder or a holy person, آپ ( āp ) is almost universally used, however تُو ( tū ) may also very rarely (chiefly in India) be used for an elder relative to show intimacy. Note that poetically, تُو ( tū ) is frequently used for any person regardless of status.

 Like cognates of this term in various languages, the abstract monotheistic God is addressed as تُو ( tū ) . This is used to signify intimacy with Him, as well as to emphasise His theological oneness (as تُو ( tū ) is exclusively singular). An exception, however, is God in Hinduism , who is more commonly addressed as تُم ( tum ) or آپ ( āp ) , as are polytheistic deities in Hinduism and other religions.
