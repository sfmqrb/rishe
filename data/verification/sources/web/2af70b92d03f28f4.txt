 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 جا • ( jā ) I ( non-past يجي ( yji ) or ييجي ( yīji ) ) 

 to come 
 جيتي انت وياه لعندنا. ― jīti nta w-iyyāh l-ʕandna ― You and he came to us. 

 to suit ( to be suitable or apt for one's image ) [ with مع ]
 جا معاك هاد السروال. ― ja mʕāk hād es-sirwāl ― Those pants suit you. 

 to arrive 
 Synonym: وصل ( wṣal ) 

 ( in the past tense ) to be located 
 فين جات مصر؟ ― fīn jāt miṣr ― Where is Egypt located? 

 ( in the present tense ) to be related to someone
 واش كتجيك ديك البنت؟ ― wāš katjīk dīk el-bint? ― Is that girl related to you? 
 كنجيه شي حاجة. ― kanjīh ši ḥāja. ― I am related to him somehow

 Tunisian Arabic
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Arabic جَاءَ ( jāʔa ) . Cognate with Maltese ġie .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 IPA ( key ) : [ʒe̞] 

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 جا ( je ) 

 to come , to arrive 
 Antonym: مشَى ( mše ) 

 to proceede 
 جَا بِاش يحِلّ البَاب ― jē bēš yḥell l-bēb ― He proceeded to open the door 

 sets a condition that waits to be triggered. Preceded by كِان and often followed by a present participle or an adjective : to be , were 
 Synonym: بدِا ( bde ) 
 كِان جِاوا خادمِين علَى روِاحهُم، خِير kēn jēw ḵādmīn ʕlə rwēḥhom, ḵīr If they were hard-working, it would've been fairer 

 كِان جِيت لِابِاس عِلِيَّ، رَانِي دُرتهَا الدِّنيَا kēn jīt lebēs ʕleyyə rāni dorthə d-denyə If I were rich, I would've toured the world 

 ( auxiliary , passive voice ) to be + past participle 
 جِا مَبطُوح ― jē mabtūḥ ― He was thrown [over the floor] 
 Note that this is equivalent to prefixing the verb with passive particle ت 

 ( genealogical ) (followed by ل ) to look like , to enjoy similar ( physical ) features 
 جِا ل بُوه je l -būh He (the offspring) looks like his father 

 to sum up 
 هِاذُومَا يجِيو عَشرَة وتلَاثَة hēðūmə yjīw ʕašra w-tlēṯə Those sum up to Dt10.3 (literally, “ those come ten and three ”) 

 to be possible , appropriate or acceptable , to suit 
 مَا يجِي ش! ― mə yjī š! ― This is un acceptable ! 
 كِان السپِادرِي مَا جَا كِش، خَلِّيهُولِي بِاللهِ kēn s-spēdri mə jē keš, ḵallīhouli bellēhi If the sneakers didn't suit you, please save them for me 

 to land , would be , to turn out 
 قرِيب يجِي علَى وِجهُو qrīb yjī ʕlə wejhu He almost fell facing down (literally, “ he had almost fallen on his face ”) 

 لِا، مَا تجِي ش حَارّة هَكَّاكَة le, mə tjī š ḥārra hakkēkə No, it would n't be spicy that way 

 to lie , to be located 
 "وِين تجِي البُوسنَة؟" "فِي اٌورُوپَّا" "wīn tji l-būsnə?" "fi œroppə" "Where is Bosnia located ?" "In Europe" 

 (often preceded by ل ) to attend 
 جِيت لِلحَفلَة البِارَح؟ jīt l-el-ḥæflə l-bēræḥ? Did you attend the concert, yesterday? 
 (comparable with English 'to go to [event]')

 Notes
 [ edit ] 

 Alternatives of active voice imperative forms are اَيَّا ( ayyə ) and اَيِّاو ( ayyēw ) variants of هَيَّا ( hayyə ) and هَيِّاو ( hayyēw ) respectively.

 In the South, imperative forms تعَالَ / تعَالَى ( tʕālə ) and تعَالُوا ( tʕālu ) are used instead.
