 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( ʾcydhʾk' /⁠az(i)dahāg⁠/ ) / Manichaean Middle Persian 𐫀𐫉𐫅𐫍𐫀𐫃 ( ʾzdhʾg /⁠azdahāg⁠/ ) , from Avestan 𐬀𐬲𐬌⸱𐬛𐬀𐬵𐬁𐬐𐬀 ( aži.dahāka , “ Azhi Dahaka ” ) , name of a mythical serpent in Zoroastrianism, from 𐬀𐬲𐬌 ( aži , “ snake; dragon ” ) , from Proto-Iranian *Háǰiš , from Proto-Indo-Iranian *Háǰʰiš , from Proto-Indo-European *h₁ógʷʰis, *h₃égʷʰis ( “ snake ” ) . Related to the name of the Shahnameh character, ضحاک ( Zahhâk ) . Compare Manichaean Parthian 𐫀𐫋𐫅𐫍𐫀𐫃 ( ʾjdhʾg /⁠aždahāg⁠/ ) and Old Armenian Աժդահակ ( Aždahak ) . This etymology is incomplete. You can help Wiktionary by elaborating on the origins of this term.
