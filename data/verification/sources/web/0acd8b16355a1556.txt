 English

 English Wikipedia has an article on: castrum 

 Wikipedia 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Latin castrum . Doublet of Caister , Chester , ksar , and qasr .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 castrum ( plural castra ) 

 ( historical ) Among the Ancient Romans, a building or plot of land used as a military defensive position .

 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Latin Wikipedia has an article on: castrum

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Italic *kastrom , from Proto-Indo-European *ḱes- ( “ to cut, cut off, separate ” ) +‎ Proto-Indo-European *-trom (Latin -trum ) (Watkins, 1969). [ 1 ] Cognate with Sanskrit शस्त्र ( śastrá , “ cutting tool, sword ” ) . [ 2 ] See also castrō , careō .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈkas.trũː] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈkas.trum] 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 castrum   n ( genitive castrī ) ; second declension 

 castle , fort , fortress 

 ( chiefly in the plural ) several soldiers ' tents situated together ; hence, a military camp , an encampment 
 c. 52 BCE , Julius Caesar , Commentarii de bello Gallico 5.9 : Caesar exposito exercitu et loco castris idoneo capto [ … ] Caesar, having disembarked his army and chosen a convenient place for the camp [ … ] 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 Use in singular is rarer than that of castellum .
