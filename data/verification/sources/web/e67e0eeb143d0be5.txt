 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Austroasiatic *ŋaːm der. ? 
 Proto-Dravidian *nāram 

 Proto-Dravidian *kāy 

 Sanskrit नारङ्ग 

 Borrowed from Dravidian ; [ 1 ] compare Tamil நார்த்தங்காய் ( nārttaṅkāy , “ citron ” ) (itself a compound of நரந்தம் ( narantam , “ fragrance ” ) and காய் ( kāy , “ fruit ” ) ), as well as Tamil நாரங்காய் ( nāraṅkāy ) , Telugu నారంగము ( nāraṅgamu ) , Malayalam നാരങ്ങ ( nāraṅṅa ) , Kannada ನಾರಂಗಿ ( nāraṅgi ) . Attested from ca. the 4th century ( Sushruta Samhita ).

 Possibly even further derived from Proto-Austroasiatic *ŋaːm ( “ sweet ” ) (Shorto 2006, #1322, Schuessler, 2007). Compare Proto-Bahnaric: *ʔŋaːm (Sidwell, 2011) and Proto-Khasian: *ŋiam (Sidwell, 2012).

 An older theory is derivation from नागरङ्ग ( nāga-raṅga , “ coloured like lead oxide ” ) , [ 2 ] which is probably a later folk etymology . [ 1 ]
