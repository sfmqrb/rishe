 English
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Learned borrowing from Latin testis .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 Audio ( US ) : ( file ) 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 testis ( plural testes ) 

 ( anatomy ) A testicle of a vertebrate .
 1961 , Harry E. Wedeck, Dictionary of Aphrodisiacs , New York: The Citadel Press, page 14 : In the Middle Ages, for instance, a recipe for sex stimulation required the putrefied flesh of a human corpse, together with both human and animal testes and ovaries, pimento, and alcohol. 

 ( biology ) An analogous gland in invertebrates such as the hydra .

 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 For *terstis , from Proto-Italic *tristos , from Proto-Indo-European *tristh₂s ("a third party standing", after the two parties to a contract or dispute), from *tréyes ( “ three ” ) and *steh₂- ( “ to stand ” ) . Compare Oscan trstus ( “ witnesses ” , nominative plural ) . [ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] [ 4 ] [ 5 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈtɛs.tɪs] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈtɛs.tis] 

 Noun

 testis   m or f ( genitive testis ) ; third declension 

 witness 
 Synonyms: arbiter , auctor

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 Of disputed origin, perhaps either:

 related to testa ( “ pot, shell ” ) ; or [ 1 ] 

 from some particular use of testis ( “ witness ” ) , as in "bearing witness to the sex act". [ 2 ]

 [ edit ] 

 testis   m ( genitive testis ) ; third declension 

 testicle 
 c. 206 BCE , Plautus , Miles Gloriosus 5.1.34 : Si posthac prehendero ego te hīc, carebis testibus . If, after this time, I catch you here again, you will be missing your testicles .
