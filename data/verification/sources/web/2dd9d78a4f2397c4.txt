 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian 𐭪𐭱𐭥𐭥 ( kišwar ) , from Avestan 𐬐𐬀𐬭𐬀𐬱𐬀𐬎𐬎𐬀𐬭𐬀 ( karšvar , “ realm, region; one of the seven realms of the world ” ) , from the root 𐬐𐬀𐬭𐬀𐬱𐬀 ( karš- , “ to plough ” ) (the same root as کشیدن ( kašidan , “ to pull, draw, stretch ” ) , hence "a tract of land bordered by a ploughed line".

 The term, a Middle Persian ecclesiastical borrowing from Avestan , originally referred to the "seven realms of the (known) world" to the ancient Iranic people, which was virtually only the ancient Indo-Iranian world of Inner Asia . With the expansion of knowledge of the world during the Middle Iranian era, the term expanded to refer to more broader regions. With the secularization of the term following the rise of Islam in Persia , it came to refer to realms, countries, or territories more generally.

 Compare Central Kurdish کیشوه‌ر ( kîşwer , “ continent ” ) , Sogdian 𐫐𐫏𐫢𐫛 ( kyšp /⁠kišp⁠/ , “ country ” ) , and Parthian [script needed] ( kišfar ) .

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Classical Persian کِشْوَر ( kišwar , “ realm, region; country; kingdom, territory; clime ” ) , from Middle Persian 𐭪𐭱𐭥𐭥 ( kišwar ) , from Avestan 𐬐𐬀𐬭𐬀𐬱𐬀𐬎𐬎𐬀𐬭𐬀 ( karšvar , “ realm, region; one of the seven realms of the world ” ) , from the root 𐬐𐬀𐬭𐬀𐬱𐬀 ( karš- , “ to plough ” ) 

 Cognate Pashto کِشْوَر ( kišwár ) , Central Kurdish کیشوه‌ر ( kîşwer , “ continent ” ) , and Sogdian [script needed] ( kyšp /⁠kišp⁠/ , “ country ” ) .
