 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Italic *supemos , from Proto-Indo-European *(s)úp-m̥mo-s , from *upó + *-m̥mo- . Cognate with Proto-Germanic *ufumô , Sanskrit उपम ( upamá , “ uppermost ” ) . See also suprēmus and -issimus . [ 1 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈsʊm.mʊs] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈsum.mus] 

 Adjective
 [ edit ] 

 summus ( superlative , feminine summa , neuter summum , comparative superior , adverb summē or summiter ) ; first / second declension 

 superlative degree of superus 
 Synonym: supērus 
 Antonyms: īnfimus , īmus 
 highest 

 greatest , best , utmost 
 4th century, St Jerome , Vulgate , Tobit 3:24
 in illo tempore exauditae sunt preces amborum in conspectu gloriae summi Dei
 At that time, the prayers of them both were heard in the sight of the glory of God most high 

 ( denoting a part from a bigger whole ) the highest part of, the top of, uppermost of
 summus mōns ― the top of a/the mountain 
 summa columna ― the top of a column 
 summa senectūs ― very old age ("the top" of old age)
