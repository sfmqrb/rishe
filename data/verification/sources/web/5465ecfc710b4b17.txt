 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Italian spècie , from Latin speciēs . Compare German Spezies , French espèce . Related to specifa ( “ specific ” ) . Doublet of spico .

 Latin

 Alternative forms

 spiciō 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Proto-Italic *spekjō , from Proto-Indo-European *spéḱyeti . [ 1 ] 

 Cognates include Ancient Greek σκέπτομαι ( sképtomai ) , Avestan 𐬯𐬞𐬀𐬯𐬫𐬈𐬌𐬙𐬌 ( spasye i ti ) , and Sanskrit पश्यति ( páśyati ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈspɛ.ki.oː] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈspɛː.t͡ʃi.o] 

 Hyphenation: spe‧ci‧ō 

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 speciō ( present infinitive specere , perfect active spexī , supine spectum ) ; third ( -iō variant) conjugation 

 ( Old Latin , rare ) to observe , watch , look at 
 Synonyms: cōnspicor , īnspectō , īnspiciō , intueor , invīsō , spectō , suspiciō , tueor , videō , vīsō 
 116 BCE – 27 BCE , Marcus Terentius Varro , De Lingua Latina 82 : Spectare dictum ab antiquo, quo etiam Ennius usus: "Quos Epulo postquam spexit " . . . et quod in auguriis etiam nunc augures dicunt avem specere . Consuetudo com<m>unis quae cum praeverbi<i>s coniun<c>ta fuerunt etiam nunc servat, ut aspicio, conspicio, respicio, suspicio, despicio Spectare derives from an old word, which Ennius also used: "After Epulo saw them" . . . and because even now, in the taking of auguries, some augurs say specere [to watch ] a bird. Common custom still preserves compounds joined with preverbs, such as aspicio , conspicio , respicio , suspicio , despicio 

 c. 2nd century , Sextus Pompeius Festus , De Verborum Significatione 446.2–5: spicit quoque sine praepositione dixerunt antiqui . . . et spexit . Ennius lib. XVI: quos ubi rex Epulo spexit de cotibus celsis The ancients also used to say spicit [ sees ] without a preposition and spexit [ saw ]. Ennius. Book 19: "When the king Epulo saw them from the high rocks"
