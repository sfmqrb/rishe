 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Proto-Indo-Aryan *śūcī́- , and cognate with Persian سوزن ( sōzan , “ needle ” ) ; the palatal fricative ś was changed to an alveolar s , perhaps from contamination with स्यू ( syū , “ alternative form of सिव् ( siv , “ to sew ” ) ” ) . The Indo-Aryan root is further derived from Proto-Indo-European *ḱeh₃- ( “ to sharpen ” ) , and connected with शूल ( śūla , “ lance, stake ” ) ; see there for more cognates.

 [ edit ]

 ( Classical Sanskrit ) IPA ( key ) : /s̪uː.t͡ɕiː/ 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 सू॒ची • ( sūcī́ ) stem ,  f
