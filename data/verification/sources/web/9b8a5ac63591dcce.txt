 This Proto-Germanic entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Germanic
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. 

 The term is sometimes connected with Avestan 𐬯𐬙𐬀𐬊𐬭𐬀 ( staora , “ large domestic animal ” ) , which could perhaps indicate a PIE form *stéh₂wros ( “ big ” ) , though a pre-form of this exact shape is also suggested to be the source of Proto-Germanic *stōraz . [ 1 ] 

 The EIEC reconstructs a shared pre-form *stewros ( “ large (domestic) animal ” ) and presumes that the semantics of the Germanic terms were influenced by *táwros ( “ bull ” ) , [ 2 ] whence also Proto-Germanic *þeuraz ( “ bull ” ) .

 Alternatively, the term may instead have been inherited from the same source as Proto-Germanic *þeuraz ( “ bull ” ) , from Proto-Indo-European *táwros , though Kroonen doubts this possibility, arguing that it requires ablaut with the non-IE word *táwros (which is probably from a substrate source) and also s-mobile. [ 1 ] 

 Ultimately, Kroonen suggests that both *steuraz and *þeuraz were borrowed from a substrate source with "irregular sound substitution" of a non-IE consonant, thereby producing the two variant forms. [ 1 ] As an example of the possible non-IE phoneme in question, Kroonen suggests /θ/ , [ 1 ] which does appear in Etruscan θevrumines ( “ minotaur ” ) . [ 3 ] Kroonen has further proposed that the Germanic forms may have been borrowed twice at different stages of the language, in which case the term *steuraz was perhaps borrowed before Grimm's law and thus before the language had dental fricatives. Alternatively, Kroonen postulates that the differing initial consonants could merely reflect a series of sound substitutions as the wanderwort spread across various languages. [ 4 ]
