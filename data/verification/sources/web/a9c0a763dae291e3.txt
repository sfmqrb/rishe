 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 FWOTD – 10 June 2014 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Indo-European *h₂wortéh₂ , from *h₂wer- (whence ἀείρω ( aeírō , “ to raise, lift, hang ” ) ).

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 IPA ( key ) : /a.or.tɛ̌ː/ → /a.orˈti/ → /a.orˈti/ 

 ( 5 th BCE Attic ) IPA ( key ) : /a.or.tɛ̌ː/ 

 ( 1 st CE Egyptian ) IPA ( key ) : /a.orˈte̝/ 

 ( 4 th CE Koine ) IPA ( key ) : /a.orˈti/ 

 ( 10 th CE Medieval ) IPA ( key ) : /a.orˈti/ 

 ( 15 th CE Constantinopolitan ) IPA ( key ) : /a.orˈti/ 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 ᾰ̓ορτή • ( ăortḗ )  f ( genitive ᾰ̓ορτῆς ) ; first declension 

 ( in the plural ) the lower extremities of the windpipe 
 460 BCE – 370 BCE , Hippocrates of Kos , de locis 415

 ( in the singular ) aorta , the artery which proceeds from the left ventricle
 384 BCE – 322 BCE , Aristotle , History of Animals 1.17.14 

 384 BCE – 322 BCE , Aristotle , History of Animals 3.3.7 

 384 BCE – 322 BCE , Aristotle , History of Animals 3.5.1 

 ( in the plural ) the arteries 
 c. 177 CE , Pollux , Onomasticon 2.205

 a knapsack that hangs from the shoulders
 342 BCE – 290 BCE , Menander , The Woman-Hater 11

 342 BCE – 291 BCE , Diphilus , The Claimant 1

 316 BCE – 250 BCE , Posidippus Comicus , Harmost 1
