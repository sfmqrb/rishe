 Reconstruction notes
 [ edit ] 

 Kloekhorst argues that the basic shape of the word must be reconstructed as *HeH-es- , and one of these laryngeals must have been *h₃ so as to explain the o -vowel in the Latin descendant. Since the PIE sequence *h₃e- should yield ḫ- in Hittite, Kloekhorst suggests that the initial laryngeal must have been *h₁ . [ 1 ] Likewise, Derksen argues that the Baltic evidence necessitates a PIE pre-form of the shape *Heh₃-s- , also with the *h₃ as the second laryngeal. [ 2 ] 

 The Hittite forms point to two separate possible inflectional paradigms within PIE. Kloekhorst argues that a nominative singular form *h₁éh₃-s should yield Hittite **āš , which could perhaps have shifted to 𒀀𒄿𒅖 ( a-i-iš /⁠aiš⁠⁠/ ) due to the inflection of the oblique stem. This theory would require said stem to take the shape *h₁h₃-és- . However, the geminate -šš- in the Hittite oblique cases requires a PIE pre-form *h₁h₃-s- , which itself would indicate hysterodynamic inflection. [ 1 ] Kroonen argues that the Verner alternation between *ōs- ~ *ōz- in Germanic indicates a PIE pre-form *(h₁/h₃)é(h₁/h₃)s ~ *(h₁/h₃)(h₁/h₃)-es-ós . [ 3 ] 

 Melchert, however, provides an entirely distinct proposal. He argues that the geminate sequence *-šš- can only be explained by a PIE form *(H)VHs- , thereby supposedly disproving the existence of a weak stem *HHes- . In such a scenario, both laryngeals would be adjacent to the vowel, meaning that—if there was an *h₃ laryngeal—it would have colored the adjacent vowel to [o] , yet Melchert argues that the Hittite i -vocalism requires pretonic *e in the weak stem. Thus, Melchert concludes that the original PIE word must have had an o-grade and was therefore perhaps an acrostatic root noun of the shape *(h₁)óh₁(-)s ~ *(h₁)éh₁(-)s-e/os . Melchert further proposes that his theory is capable of explaining the unusual outcome of the Hittite term. He argues that *(h₁)óh₁s could become *óhes or *óʔes , which could then become *ó_es , then *ó_is , before contracting into 𒀀𒄿𒅖 ( a-i-iš /⁠aiš⁠⁠/ ) . Moreover, Melchert notes that other PIE words for body parts, such as *dóws , are also o-grade acrostatic nouns. [ 4 ]
