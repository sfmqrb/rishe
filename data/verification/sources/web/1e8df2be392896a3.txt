 English garçon 

 From French garçon   m (1788), from Old French garçon , oblique case of gars ( “ servant ” ) , from Medieval Latin garciō   m , from Frankish *wrakkjō ( “ servant, boy ” ) , from Proto-Germanic *wrakjô ( “ exile, driven one ” ) , from Proto-Indo-European *wreg- ( “ to drive ” ) . Doublet of garson and wretch .

 Cognate with Old High German wrecheo , recko ( “ exile, warrior, hero ” ) (Modern German Recke ), Old Saxon wrekkio ( “ a banished person, exile, stranger ” ) , Old English wreċċa ( “ a wretch, stranger, exile ” ) , and perhaps to Old Norse rekkr ( “ man, warrior, hero ” ) . More at wreak .

 French
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle French garçon , from Old French garçon , garçun   m ( “ servant, boy ” ) , from Early Medieval Latin garciōnem , accusative of garciō   m ( “ mercenary, servant, boy ” ) , from Frankish *wrakkjō ( “ servant, boy ” ) , from Proto-Germanic *wrakjô ( “ exile, driven one ” ) . Cognate with Modern German Recke ("warrior"), Old English wreċċa ( “ exile, outcast ” ) , whence Modern English wretch .
