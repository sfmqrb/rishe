 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 From Aramaic קָזָא / קָאזָא ( qāzzā , “ floss silk , cotton - seed ” ) from Middle Persian [script needed] ( kač ) (New Persian کژ ( kaž ) ), which has also been borrowed directly as خَزّ ( ḵazz ) . See the New Persian entry for more.

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Arabic قَصَّ ( qaṣṣa , “ to trace, to follow someone's tracks ” ) , with /sˤ/ → /z/ as in زَيّ ( zayy , “ like ” ) . The semantic development from "following/tracing" to "gazing" arose from the act of watching someone from a distance while pursuing them; compare Qur'an (in Arabic), 609–632 CE , 28:11 : “ فَبَصُرَتْ بِهِ عَن جُنُبٍ ― fabaṣurat bihi ʕan junubin ― so she watched him from a distance ” .

 The derivative قَزَّر ( gazzar , “ to gaze for a long time ” ) shows a root-level ⟨ ر ⟩ suffix indicating repetition, suggesting the word is inherited rather than borrowed.

 Alternatively from جَسَّ ( jassa , “ to examine, to look at intensely ” ) . Surface similarity to English gaze is coincidental.

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 قز • ( gazz ) I ( non-past يقزّ ( yigizz ) ) 

 to watch someone

 to follow someone

 to gaze / stare at someone from a distance while following them
 Synonyms: شاف ( šāf , “ to see ” ) ; برّق ( barrag / barrad͡z , “ to glare ” ) ; شمّق ( šammag , “ side-eyeing, look downly ” ) , بحّر ( baḥḥar , “ to stare too long ” ) , ناظر ( nāẓar , “ to watch ” ) 
 قَام يقز بهالبنات ― gām yigizz b-hal-banāt ― he started gazing at these girls 

 to potter and waste time by following something unproductively, or with no purpose
 Synonyms: فرفر ( farfar ) , سعسع ( saʕsaʕ ) , سعسق ( saʕsag / saʕsad͡z )
