 This Proto-Germanic entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Germanic
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. Likely a Wanderwort of non-Indo-European substrate origin. [ 1 ] Compare Proto-Balto-Slavic *sirabras ( “ silver ” ) , Basque zilar ( “ silver ” ) , Celtiberian silabur ( “ money ” ) , [ 2 ] and possibly Ancient Greek σῐ́δηρος ( sĭ́dēros , “ iron ” ) . A Semitic origin has also been proposed, comparing Proto-Berber *a-ẓrəf ( “ silver ” ) , Akkadian 𒀫𒁍𒌝 ( ṣur-pu-um /⁠ṣurpum⁠/ , “ silver ” ) , 𒍝𒊏𒁍𒌝 ( ṣa-ra-pu-um /⁠ṣarāpum⁠/ , “ to smelt, refine ” ) ; however, this derivation has been rejected by some scholars [ 3 ] as phonologically and geographically implausible.

 The displacement of unattested Proto-Germanic *urkundą , from Proto-Indo-European *h₂ŕ̥ǵn̥tom ( “ silver ” ) , has been connected with the adoption of more advanced metallurgical techniques and terminology. [ 4 ]
