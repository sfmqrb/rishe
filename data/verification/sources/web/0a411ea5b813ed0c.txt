 As described in the 9th century Zoroastrian text Dadestan-i Denig ("Religious Decisions"), [ 1 ] hamistagan or hamēstagān is a neutral place or state for the departed souls of those whose good deeds and bad deeds were equal in life. Here these souls await Judgment Day . Meanwhile, those who did mostly good experience bliss and those who did mostly evil suffer torment. Religious Decisions was written in Persia (modern day Iran ) when Islam was replacing Zoroastrianism as the majority religion. [ relevant? ] 

 The Encyclopædia Iranica considers this a late idea and perhaps comes from the queries of Zoroastrian theologians [ 2 ] 

 Hamistagan can be compared to Roman Catholic purgatory because it occupies a position between heaven and hell , but hamistagan is a place of waiting, not punishment and purification. As a neutral place, hamistagan is more like the Roman Catholic limbo .
