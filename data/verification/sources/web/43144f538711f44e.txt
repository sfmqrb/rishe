 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian pwšytn' ( pōšīdan , “ cover, put on, wear ” ) , [ 1 ] from Proto-Iranian *paus- ( “ to dress, clothe, cover ” ) , probably borrowed from a substrate language. [ 2 ] Cheung additionally adduces Sanskrit पवस्त ( pavásta , “ covering, garment ” ) as a potential cognate; this is unlikely, considering the Sanskrit word's Vedic presence and the existence of a plausible Indo-Iranian etymology.

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 پوشیدن • ( pōšīdan  / pušidan ) ( present stem پوش ( pōš  / puš ) , Tajik spelling пӯшидан ) 

 to put on (clothes), to wear 

 to cover 

 ( Tajik ) to close 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 While often translated as "to wear", the Persian verb refers specifically to the action of putting on clothes, whereas the English word refers both to the action and to the state of having clothes on. The Persian equivalent of English "to be wearing" is hence the present perfect of پوشیدن ( pōšīdan  / pušidan ) , literally "to have put on".
