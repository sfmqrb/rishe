 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *h₂werg- 
 Proto-Indo-European *h₂wérg-e-ti 
 Proto-Italic *wergō 
 Latin vergō 

 Inherited from Proto-Italic *wergō , from Proto-Indo-European *h₂wérg-e-ti , [ 1 ] from *h₂werg- ( “ to turn ” ) . Related to Hittite 𒄷𒌨𒆠𒅖 ( ḫu-ur-ki-iš /⁠ḫurkis⁠/ , “ wheel ” ) , Ancient Greek ἐέργω ( eérgō , “ to stop, scare away ” ) , Ancient Greek εἶρξαι ( eîrxai , “ to hold off ” ) , Sanskrit वृणक्ति ( vṛṇakti , “ to turn around, ward off ” ) , Sanskrit वरीवृजत्- ( varīvṛjat- , “ bending again and again ” , ptcp. ) , Sanskrit प्र वावृजे ( pra vāvṛje , “ is turned towards ” ) , and Tocharian A wärkṣantāñ ( “ rotating ” , ptcp. ) . [ 2 ] According to De Vaan, Latin vermina is from the same root. [ 3 ]

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 vergō ( present infinitive vergere , perfect active versī ) ; third conjugation , no supine stem 

 ( transitive , poetic ) to bend , turn , incline 
 c. 99 BCE – 55 BCE , Lucretius , De rerum natura 2.212 : in terras igitur quoque solis vergitur ardor Therefore, the sun's heat also tends towards Earth 

 12 CE – 13 CE , Ovidius , Epistulae ex Ponto 1.10.51–52 : ille tibi exequias et magni funus honoris fecit et in gelidos versit amoma sinus 1924 translation by A. L. Wheeler For you he conducted funerary rites and a funeral of great honor, and inclined the balsam towards the cold breasts (literally, “He conducted for thee a funeral with ceremonials of great honour, pouring the balsam upon thy cold breast”) 

 ( intransitive ) to bend, turn, verge , slope down
 c. 430 CE – c. 489 CE , Sidonius Apollinaris , Carmina 5.345–346 : et, se Lucrinas qua vergit Gaurus in undas, bracchia Massylus iactaret nigra natator And, at the place where Gaurus turns towards Lucrine waters, the swimmer Massylus throws about his swarthy arms 

 ( intransitive ) to incline towards a certain attitude 
 c. 58 CE , Seneca Minor , De vita beata 15.3 : Sed ne patriae quidem bonus tutor aut vindex est nec amicorum propugnator, si ad voluptates vergit But he is not even a good teacher or a vindicator of his fatherland nor a defender of his friends if he tends towards pleasures 

 ( intransitive ) to incline towards a physical characteristic 
 77 CE – 79 CE , Plinius Maior , Naturalis Historia 12.26 : colore languido in candidum vergente 1945 translation by H. Rackham a sickly color inclining to white 

 77 CE – 79 CE , Plinius Maior , Naturalis Historia 37.58 : post hos Cyprius vocatur in Cypro repertus, vergens ad aereum colorem, sed in medica vi, de qua dicemus, efficacissimus After this comes what is called the Cyprian, which is found in Cyprus and tends towards the color of copper, but has highly effective medical capabilities, which I shall describe later 

 ( intransitive ) to decline 
 c. 117 CE , Tacitus , Annales 2.43 : nec posse motum Orientem nisi Germanici sapientia conponi: nam suam aetatem vergere , Drusi nondum satis adolevisse It was not possible for the trouble in the east to resolved if not by the wisdom of Germanicus, for he was declining in his own years, and Drusus had not yet sufficently matured 

 121 CE , Suetonius , De vita Caesarum 7 7 : Dein vergente iam die ingressus senatum Afterwards, as the day was coming to an end , he entered the Senate 

 ( intransitive ) to be situated , lie 
 c. 52 BCE , Julius Caesar , Commentarii de bello Gallico 2.18.2 : Collis ab summo aequaliter declivis ad flumen Sabim, quod supra nominavimus, vergebat A rise sloping downwards evenly from the summit towards the river Sabis, which I mentioned above, was situated there
