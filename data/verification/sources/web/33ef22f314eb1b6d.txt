 Old English
 [ edit ] 

 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 neb 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-West Germanic *nabi , from Proto-Germanic *nabją . Akin to Old Norse nef , Middle Low German nebbe and Middle Dutch nebbe .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 IPA ( key ) : /nebb/ , [neb] 

 Rhymes: -ebb 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 nebb   n 

 face 
 late 10th century , Ælfric , "Saint Agnes, Virgin" 
 He gesette his tacn on minum nebbe þæt ic nænne oðerne ofer hine ne lufige. He hath set His token upon my face that I should love none other beside Him. 

 Wonders of the East 
 Ðǣr beoð akende men, ðā beoð fiftȳne fōta lange ⁊ hī habbað hwīt līċ ⁊ tū neb on ānum hēafde, fēt ⁊ cnēowu swīðe rēad, ⁊ lange nosu ⁊ sweart feax. Men are born there who are fifteen feet tall. They have white bodies, two faces on each head, feet and knees that are very red, long noses, and black hair. 

 nose 

 bill or beak 
 c. 980 , Exeter Book Riddle 91 : Hwīlum iċ under bæc breġde nebbe , hyrde þæs hordes, þonne mīn hlāford wile lāfe þiċġan þāra þe hē of līfe hēt wælcræfte āwrecan willum sīnum. Sometimes I bend bill under my back, keeper of this hoard, when my lord wishes to take the leftovers of those whom he bade to drive out of live with deadly might by his wills.
