 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Vedic ) IPA ( key ) : /ɡʱr̩/ 

 ( Classical Sanskrit ) IPA ( key ) : /ɡʱr̩/ 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. According to Whitney, this root is probably identical with घृ ( ghṛ , “ to shine ” ) , from *gʷʰer- ( “ to heat, burn ” ) . [ 1 ] Semantically, the possible derivative ghṛtá ( “ ghee, fat ” ) may have emerged from the process of heating butter to produce ghee . [ 2 ] The LIV, however, classifies jígharti as possibly belonging to a separate Proto-Indo-European root of the shape *g (ṷ)h er- . Nevertheless, according to the LIV, the existence of such a root in PIE is doubtful to a supposed lack of cognates. [ 3 ] Grestenberger suggests that Younger Avestan 𐬘𐬀𐬖𐬭𐬌 ( jaγri ) may serve as a possible Indo-Iranian cognate. [ 4 ] It is perhaps possible that term may connect to Middle Irish gert [ 5 ] and Ancient Greek μελιχρός ( melikhrós ) , upon which basis Olsen reconstructs Proto-Indo-European *gʰer- ( “ to sprinkle ” ) . [ 6 ]

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Proto-Indo-European *gʷʰer- ( “ warm, hot ” ) .

 [ edit ]
