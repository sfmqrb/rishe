 See also: Πῶρος and πωρός 

 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 It has no certain etymology. According to Haupt, it was borrowed from Akkadian ; Furnée connects *ψώρος ( *psṓros ) in ψωρίτης ( psōrítēs , “ kind of marble ” ) , suggesting a Pre-Greek origin. Likely a Wanderwort also found in Akkadian 𒉌𒌓𒈾𒁓 ( /⁠pīlu, pūlu⁠/ , “ limestone ” ) , Akkadian 𒁓𒊏 ( pūru , literally “ stone bowl ” ) , Urartian 𒉌𒌓𒁍𒇻𒋛 ( NA₄ pu-lu-si /⁠pulus⁠/ , “ stone, stele ” ) , Old Armenian բուռ ( buṙ , “ lime, plaster, varnish ” ) . An ancient Anatolian or Mesopotamian origin is probable, with Sumerian 𒁓 ( /⁠bur⁠/ , “ stone (vessel) ” ) as the likely source.

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 πῶρος • ( pôros )  m ( genitive πώρου ) ; second declension 

 tuff 

 kind of marble used in building 

 stalactite in caverns 

 ( pathology ) stone in the bladder
