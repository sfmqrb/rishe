 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-Iranian *walkás 
 Middle Persian [script needed] ( wlg ) 
 Persian برگ 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( wlg /⁠warg⁠/ , “ leaf ” ) (whence Early New Persian بلگ ( balg ) ), from Proto-Indo-Iranian *warkás ~ *walkás . Cognate with Younger Avestan 𐬬𐬀𐬭𐬆𐬐𐬀 ( var ə ka ) , Sanskrit वल्क ( valka , “ bark of a tree ” ) , Mazanderani ولگ ( valg ) .

 Dari

 Iranian Persian

 Tajik

 барг 

 برگ • ( barg ) ( plural برگ‌ها ( barg-hā / barg-hâ ) , Tajik spelling барг ) 

 leaf 
 برگ زرد و خراب barg -e zard va xarâb yellow, wilted leaf 

 برگ سبزی است تحفه درویش. barg -e sabzi ast tohfe-ye darviš. A green leaf is a poor man's gift. (Persian proverb about poverty) 

 c. 1011 , Abu'l-Qāsim Firdawsī , “The tale of Kay Qubād”, in شاهنامه [ Book of Kings ]‎ [1] : چو دانست کامد به نزدیک مرگ
بپژمرد خواهد همی سبز برگ čū dānist k-āmad ba nazdīk marg
bipižmurd xwāhad hamē sabz barg When it knows that death has come near,
The green leaf will wither away. 

 petal 
 برگ گل رز ― barg -e gol-e roz ― rose petal 

 sheet , folio

 Etymology 2
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. Perhaps a cognate of Sanskrit वल्गा ( valgā , “ bridle, rein ” ) . Old Armenian վարկ ( vark ) and Middle Georgian ვარგი ( vargi ) may be related borrowings from a Middle Iranian language.

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 برگ • ( barg ) ( plural برگ‌ها ( barg-hā / barg-hâ ) , Tajik spelling барг ) 

 tools , utensils , means 
 c. 1520 , Selim I of the Ottoman Empire , edited by Benedek Péri, The Persian Dīvān of Yavuz Sulṭān Selīm , Budapest, Hungary: Research Centre for the Humanities, Eötvös Loránd Research Network, →ISBN , page 256 : برون آمد ز خرگه یار برگ جنگ بربسته
بقصد خون صد همچون من مسکین کمر بسته birōn āmad zi xargah yār barg-i jang barbasta
ba-qasd-i xūn-i sad hamčūn man-i miskīn kamar basta The beloved has left the tent, having put on his weapons of war;
He has girdled his loins in search of the blood of a hundred wretches like me. (Classical Persian transliteration)

 Etymology 3
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. Perhaps ultimately a derivative of Proto-Indo-European *welh₁- ( “ to choose; to want ” ) and cognate with Sanskrit वर ( vára , “ wish, desire ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 برگ • ( barg ) ( plural برگ‌ها ( barg-hā / barg-hâ ) , Tajik spelling барг ) 

 intention , desire 
 c. 1520 , Selim I of the Ottoman Empire , edited by Benedek Péri, The Persian Dīvān of Yavuz Sulṭān Selīm , Budapest, Hungary: Research Centre for the Humanities, Eötvös Loránd Research Network, →ISBN , page 160 : یار گر گوید که جان ده یا برو از کوی من
عاشق آن باشد که اول برگ جان دادن کند yār gar gōyad ki jān dih yā biraw az kō-yi man
āšiq ān bāšad ki awwal barg-i jān dādan kunad If the beloved says "Give up your life or leave my alley!"
The lover is the one who first intends to give up his life. (Classical Persian transliteration) 

 vigour
