 Irish
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Old Irish cáith [ 1 ] and cognate with Scottish Gaelic càth . It perhaps further derives from Proto-Celtic *kwātis , itself possibly from Proto-Indo-European *(s)kʷoh₁t- , from a potential root *(s)kweh₁t- ( “ to shake ” ) . [ 2 ] Matasović, however, [ 2 ] notes that such a root structure would be unusual, and other authors have suggested that proposed comparanda (i.e. Latin quatiō ( “ to shake ” ) , Ancient Greek πάσσω ( pássō ) ) may reflect a substrate source. [ 3 ] Alternatively, the EIEC prefers to connect the term with Ancient Greek πίτῡρον ( pítūron , “ bran ” ) and πήτεα ( pḗtea , “ bran ” ) , based on which it tentatively suggests a derivation from Proto-Indo-European *kʷōti- . [ 4 ]
