 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian ʾlcwk ( ārzōg , “ desire, lust ” ) , from Proto-Iranian *rauǰ , *rauč ( “ to desire, please ” ) , of unclear further origin. [ 1 ] There may be a connection to Proto-Indo-European *lewk- ( “ to shine ” ) , as PIE *l > r and *k > *c (palatal) or š in Iranian contexts.

 آرزو • ( âr(e)zu or âr(i)zô ) ( plural آرزوها ) 

 desire 
 c. 1260s , Jalāl ad-Dīn Mohammad Rūmī , translated by Reynold A. Nicholson , مثنوی معنوی [ Masnavi-ye-Ma'navi ], volume I, verse 3969: همچنان که آرزو ی سود هست
 آرزو ی مرگ بردن ز آن به است hamčunān ki ārzō -i sud hast
 ārzō -i marg burdan zi ān bih ast Even as there is desire for profit,
the desire to win death is better than that.
