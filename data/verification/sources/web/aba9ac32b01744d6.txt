 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From root ش ر م ( š r m ) related to tearing, cleaving and wounding.

 [ edit ]

 to tear , to rip

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 شَرْم • ( šarm )  m ( plural شُرُوم ( šurūm ) ) 

 a cleave , a split 

 an abyss , ocean trench 

 bay , gulf 
 Synonyms: خَلِيج ( ḵalīj ) , جون ( jōn ) 
 شَرْمُ ٱلشَّيْخِ ― šarmu š-šayḵi ― 'The Bay of the Sheikh', known in Egyptian as Sharm El Sheikh 

 fissure in a mountain that does not pass through

 Etymology

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *pḱer- der. 
 Proto-Iranian *fšarma- 
 Middle Persian 𐭱𐭥𐭬 ( šʿm /⁠šarm⁠/ ) 
 Persian شرم 

 From Middle Persian 𐭱𐭥𐭬 ( šʿm /⁠šarm⁠/ ) , from Proto-Iranian *fšarma- ( “ shame ” ) , from *fšar- ( “ to shame, be ashamed ” ) , [ 1 ] from Proto-Indo-European *pḱormo- ( “ shame, suffering, pain ” ) , from *pḱer- ( “ to suffer, feel grief and shame ” ) . [ 2 ] Cognate with Khotanese [script needed] ( kṣär- , “ be ashamed ” ) , Avestan 𐬟𐬱𐬀𐬭𐬆𐬨𐬀 ( fšar ə ma , “ shame ” ) , Proto-Slavic *sȏrmъ ( “ shame ” ) , and English harm . [ 3 ] [ 4 ]
