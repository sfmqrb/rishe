 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Uncertain. [ 1 ] Possibly related to Lithuanian i̇̀rti ( “ to dissolve, fall into ruin ” ) and Proto-Slavic *oriti . It has also been compared to Ancient Greek ἐρῆμος ( erêmos , “ lonely ” ) , but this may be phonetically difficult if certain sound laws involving laryngeals and *r are accepted.

 More information

 Beekes 2010 and Schrijver 1991 assume Greek ἐρῆμος ( erêmos ) must be from a root of the form *h₁reh₁- . [ 2 ] [ 3 ] Schrijver 1991 therefore denies that ἐρῆμος ( erêmos ) and rārus are cognates, because per Schrijver's sound laws, no gradation of *h₁reh₁- can regularly give rise to Latin rār- : *h₁r̥h₁rós would give Latin *rarus with short -a- (compare ratus from *h₂rh₁tós ), whereas *h₁reh₁rós would give Latin * rērus with long -ē- . Schrijver is uncertain whether i̇̀rti and *oriti are cognate with Greek ἐρῆμος ( erêmos ) or with Latin rārus : in the latter case, Schrijver assumes Latin rā- came from *Hreh₂- , with schwebeablaut . Beekes treats derivation of rārus from zero-grade *h₁r̥h₁-ro- as possible, without explicitly addressing Schrijver's argument that this should be expected to yield *rar- .

 Woodhouse 2011 postulates different sound laws, assuming that Greek initial ἐρ- ( er- ) can come either from PIE *h₁r- or PIE *r- and that PIE *rHC- became Latin ra- with short -a- , whereas PIE *HrHC- became Latin rā- with long -ā- . Based on these assumptions, Woodhouse derives ratus from a root starting with *r- (instead of *h₁r as postulated by Schrijver) and assumes that Latin rārus might show rā- as the regular outcome of earlier *h₂rH- , from the zero-grade of a root *h₂erH- shared with i̇̀rti . [ 4 ] De Vaan 2008 mentions *h₂erH- ( “ to disappear, perish ” ) as a possible root of rārus but dismisses it as semantically unlikely. [ 1 ]
