 Ancient Greek
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Like many words ending in -ῠ ( -ŭ ) , it is a loanword . Furnée compares κοτῠ́λη ( kotŭ́lē , “ cup ” ) , suggesting a Pre-Greek origin, while Szemerényi refers to late Neo-Babylonian 𒃶𒁺 ( kandu , “ vessel ” ) which is considered a West Semitic borrowing from Aramaic כנדא ( kandā , “ vessel, pot; specifically for wine ” ) from Sanskrit कन्दु ( kandu , “ pot ” ) and ultimately Proto-South Dravidian *kiṇṭV- ( “ pot ” ) , found also in the forms כַּד ( kaḏ ) , כדא ( kaddā ) , Biblical Hebrew כַּד ( kad ) , Punic 𐤊𐤃 ( kd ) , Ugaritic 𐎋𐎄 ( kd ) , and Ancient Greek κᾰ́δος ( kắdos ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 κόνδῠ • ( kóndŭ )  n ( genitive κόνδῠος ) ; third declension 

 a kind of drinking vessel 

 a unit of volume derived from the vessel
