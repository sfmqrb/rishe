 Ottoman Turkish
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Persian خدیو ( xadêw, xedêw , “ lord, master, king ” ) , from Bactrian χοαδηο ( khoadēo , “ lord ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 خدیو • ( hıdiv or hediv ) 

 ( historical ) khedive , the title of Ottoman viceroy of Egypt from 1805–1914.
 1895 August 13, caption of cover photo of Servet-i Fünun , page 305:
 خدیو مصر فخامتلو دولتلو عباس حلمی پاشا حضرتلری hediv -i Mısır fehametli devletli Abbas Hilmi paşa hazretleri His highness Abbas Hilmi Pasha , khedive of Egypt 

 Descendants
 [ edit ] 

 Turkish: hidiv 

 → Arabic: خديوي ( ḵidaywiyy ) 

 → French: khédive 
 → English: khedive 

 Further reading
 [ edit ] 

 Redhouse, James W. ( 1890 ), “ خدیو ”, in A Turkish and English Lexicon ‎ [1] , Constantinople: A. H. Boyajian, page 834

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian 𐭤𐭲𐭩𐭥 ( htyw /⁠xadēw⁠/ ) , from Bactrian χοαδηο ( khoadēo , “ lord ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 خدیو • ( xadēw / xadiv ) ( plural خدیوان ( xadēwān / xadivân ) , Tajik spelling хадев ) 

 ( archaic ) lord , master , mighty ruler , king 
 10 th Century CE , Ferdowsi , Shahnameh 
 سیامک به دست خزروان دیو 
 تبه گشت و ماند انجمن بی‌ خدیو siyâmak be daste xazarvan-e div 
 tabah gašt o mând anjoman bi xadiv Siamak perished in the fight with Khazarwan Div, 
 and the people lost their lord . 

 ( historical ) khedive

 Urdu
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Classical Persian خدیو ( xadēw , “ lord; king; mighty ruler ” ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Standard Urdu ) IPA ( key ) : /xɪ.d̪eːʋ/ , /xɪ.d̪iːʋ/ , /xə.d̪eːʋ/ 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 خدیو • ( xidev or xadev )  m ( Hindi spelling ख़देव or ख़िदेव ) 

 lord , king ; ruler 

 satrap ; rich and powerful viceroy 

 master
