 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle Persian [Term?] ( /⁠âšnūdan⁠/ , “ to hear, understand ” ) , [ 1 ] from Proto-Iranian *ā- + *xšnaw- ( “ to hear; to sharpen the ears ” ) . The prefix is from Proto-Indo-European *h₂éd ( “ at ” ) , while the root is from Proto-Indo-Iranian *kšnaw- , from Proto-Indo-European *ksnew- ( “ to scrape, sharpen ” ) . [ 2 ] Compare Central Kurdish ژنفتن ( žiniftin ) (in Sanandaj ), Zazaki eşnawitiş ( “ to hear ” ) , Northern Luri اشنںفتێ ( ešnəftı ) .

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 شنیدن • ( šunīdan  / šenidan ) ( present stem شنو ( šinaw  / šenov ) , Tajik spelling шунидан ) 

 to hear 
 دیدم و ن شنیدم شان didam o na- šenidam -ešan. I saw and didn't hear them. 

 c. 1260s , Jalāl ad-Dīn Mohammad Rūmī , translated by Reynold A. Nicholson , مثنوی معنوی [ Masnavi-ye-Ma'navi ], volume III, verse 69: آن شنیدی تو که در هندوستان
دید دانایی گروهی دوستان ān šunīdī ki dar hindūstān
dīd dānāyē gurōhē dōstān Have you heard that in Hindustan,
A wise man saw a group of friends? ( Classical Persian transliteration ) 

 to listen 
 Synonyms: گوش کردن ( gōš kardan  / guš kardan ) , گوش دادن ( gōš dādan  / guš dâdan ) , نیوشیدن ( niyōšīdan  / niyušidan ) 

 to smell 
 Synonym: بوییدن ( bōyīdan  / buyidan ) 
 c. 1390 , Shams-ud-Dīn Muḥammad Ḥāfiẓ , “Ghazal 322”, in دیوان حافظ [ The Divān of Ḥāfiẓ ]‎ [1] : ز کوی یار بیار ای نسیم صبح غباری
که بوی خون دل ریش از آن تراب شنیدم zi kōy-i yār biyār ay nasīm-i subh-i ġubārī
ki bōy-i xūn-i dil-i rīš az ān turāb šunīdam Come out from the beloved's alley, o dusty morning breeze!
For I have smelled the scent of a wounded heart's blood from that soil. (Classical Persian transliteration) 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 In spoken Persian, شنیدن ( šunīdan ／ šenidan ) and شنفتن ( šunuftan ／ šenoftan ) can be used interchangeably.
