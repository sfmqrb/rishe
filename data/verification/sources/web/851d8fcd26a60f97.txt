 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *k⁽ʷ⁾yéwetor 
 Proto-Indo-Iranian *čyáwatay 
 Persian شدن 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [script needed] ( OZLWN-tn' /⁠šudan⁠/ ) , from Old Persian 𐎠𐏁𐎡𐎹𐎺𐎶 ( š(i)yav- , “ to set, go forth ” ) , from Proto-Indo-Iranian *čyáwatay , from Proto-Indo-European *kyew- ( “ to move, go ” ) . Cognates include Northern Kurdish çûn ( “ to go ” ) , Ossetian цӕуын ( cæwyn ) , Pashto شول ( šwəl ) , Sanskrit च्यवते ( cyávate , “ move to and fro, stir ” ) , Old Armenian չու ( čʻu ) and Ancient Greek σεύω ( seúō , “ put in quick motion, drive ” ) . Note Old Armenian արշաւեմ ( aršawem ) , ապաշաւ ( apašaw ) , all borrowed from Iranian.

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 شدن • ( šudan  / šodan ) ( present stem شَو ( šaw  / šov ) , Tajik spelling шудан ) 

 to become ; a light verb used in a large number of intransitive compound verbs. 
 Coordinate term: کردن ( kardan ) 
 آب شدن ― âb šodan ― to melt (literally, “ to become water ”) 
 بیدار شدن ― bidâr šodan ― to wake up (literally, “ to become awake ”) 
 او پادشاه شد . ― u pâdešâh šod . ― He became king. 

 ( in passive constructions ) to be 
 ساخته شد ― sâxte šod ― it was built 

 ( in the third person singular ) to be possible ; can (in contexts of permission, etc., and not inner ability)
 اگر بشود ― agar bešavad ― if it's possible 
 می‌شه منم برم؟ ( colloquial Iranian ) ― mi-še man-am beram? ― Can I go too? 
 می‌شد رفت. ― mi-šod raft. ― It was possible to go. 
 می‌شد او را ببینم. ― mi-šod u râ bebinam. ― I could see him. 

 ( mathematics , informal ) to equal ; to make 

 ( archaic , poetic ) to go , to come to 

 Usage notes
 [ edit ] 

 شدن is usually used with nouns and adjectives to produce intransitive verbs. However, when used with a noun which is a present participle, it can produce transitive verbs, as in یادآور شدن ( yâd-âvar šodan , “ to remind, to notify ” , literally “ to become the reminder [of] ” ) or مرتکب شدن ( mortakeb šodan , “ to perpetrate (a crime) ” , literally “ to become the perpetrator [of] ” ) .
