 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 مَرَدَ • ( marada ) I ( non-past يَمْرُدُ ( yamrudu ) , verbal noun مَرَادَة ( marāda ) or مُرُودَة ( murūda ) ) 

 to be bold 

 to be rebellious 

 to be accustomed [ with عَلَى ( ʕalā ) ‘to something’ ]

 to suckle 

 to disgrace (someone)

 Etymology 4
 [ edit ] 

 Unknown, possibly from a Middle Iranian cognate of Persian مورد ( murd , “ myrtle ” ) . The “rebellion” senses of the root م ر د ( m r d ) may then be figurative after the tonic properties of the myrtle or the thermogenic, aphrodisiac and purgative effects of the toothbrush tree fruits.

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( mlt' /⁠mard⁠/ ) , [Book Pahlavi needed] ( GBRA /⁠mard⁠/ ) , from Old Persian 𐎶𐎼𐎫𐎡𐎹 ( martiya ) , from Proto-Iranian *mŕ̥tah , from Proto-Indo-Iranian *mŕ̥tas , and ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *mr̥tós ( “ dead, mortal ” ) , from *mer- ( “ to die ” ) ; whence also مردن ( mordan , “ to die ” ) , and مردم ( mardom , “ people ” ) . [ 1 ] Noun sense 2 is a semantic loan from English man .

 Cognates include Northern Kurdish mêr ( “ husband ” ) , Northern Luri میره ( mırə , “ husband ” ) , Pashto مېړۀ ( meṛə , “ husband ” ) , Sanskrit मर्त ( marta , “ mortal, man ” ) , Old Armenian մարդ ( mard , “ man ” ) , Ancient Greek βροτός ( brotós , “ mortal ” ) , and Latin mortuus ( “ dead ” )

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 مرد • ( mard ) ( plural مردان ( mardān / mardân ) , or مردها ( mard-hā / mard-hâ ) , Tajik spelling мард ) 

 man ( adult male human)
 یک مردِ جوان ― yek mard -e javan ― A young man 

 ( in the vocative ) used to address a man 
 چطوری مرد ؟ ― četowri mard ? ― How are you doing, man ? 

 a member of a team or group , usually in plural
 یوآخیم لو و مردانش برزیل را هفت بر یک شکست دادند. yu-âxim low û mardânaš berezil râ haft bar yek šekast dâdand. Joachim Löw and his men beat Brazil 7-1 .
