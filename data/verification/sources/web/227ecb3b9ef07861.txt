 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian 𐭠𐭡𐭱𐭠𐭥 ( ābšār ) . By surface analysis , آب ( āb , “ water ” ) +‎ شار ( šār , “ pour ” ) , see شاریدن ( šārīdan ) for the second part. Compare Northern Kurdish avşirr , daşirr ( “ toilet ” ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 آبشار • ( ābšār / âbšâr ) ( plural آبْشَارها ( ābšār-hā ) , Tajik spelling обшор ) 

 waterfall ; cascade 
 c. 1670 , Ṣāʾib-i Tabrīzī , “Ghazal 17”, in دیوان [ Dīvān ]‎ [1] : نگسلد فریاد مرغان چمن از یکدگر
روز و شب از تردماغی چون صدای آبشار na-gusilad faryād-i murġān-i čaman az yakdigar
rōz u šab az tardimāġī čūn sadā-yi ābšār The cries of the meadow nightingales do not separate from each other
Out of mirth day and night, like the sound of the waterfall . (Classical Persian romanization)
