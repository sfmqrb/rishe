 English
 [ edit ] 

 English Wikipedia has an article on: samovar 

 Wikipedia 

 A conical urn-shaped silver-plated samovar 
 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Russian самова́р ( samovár , “ self-boiler ” ) ; from само́ ( samó , “ self ” ) + вари́ть ( varítʹ , “ boil, cook ” ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 IPA ( key ) : /ˈsæməvɑː(ɹ)/ , /ˌsæməˈvɑː(ɹ)/ 

 Audio ( US ) : ( file ) 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 samovar ( plural samovars ) 

 A metal urn with a spigot , for boiling water for making tea. Traditionally, the water is heated by hot coals or charcoal in a chimney-like tube which runs through the center of the urn. Today, it is more likely that the water is heated by an electric coil.
 1901 [ 1878 ], chapter 25, in Constance Garnett , transl., Anna Karenina ‎ [1] , translation of Анна Каренина ( Anna Karenina ) by Leo Tolstoy , part 3: The samovar was beginning to sing; the laborers and the family, having disposed of the horses, came in to dinner. 

 1919 , Ronald Firbank , Valmouth , hardback edition, Duckworth, page 107 : Come on now with the samovar ─ and make haste sorting the letter-bag. 

 1932 , Maurice Baring , chapter 20, in Friday's Business ‎ [2] : Eurydice pointed to the cupboard, and sat down on the low divan with folded hands, and looked at the floor. [ … ] Elsa made her drink a glass of vodka. Then she fetched the samovar from the kitchen, and made tea. 

 2006 October 11, Tina Fey , “ Pilot ” (2:15 from the start), in 30 Rock ‎ [3] , season 1 , episode 1, spoken by James "Toofer" Spurlock ( Keith Powell ): “Chumley. The beverage situation around here is reprehensible.” “Oh, good morning, sunshine.” “Surely our massive conglomerate parent company could spring for a samovar of coffee.” “Yeah, or like a big coffee dispenser.” “That's what a samovar is.” “Are there other black nerds, or is it just you and Urkel?”
