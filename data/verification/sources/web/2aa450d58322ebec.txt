 This Proto-Germanic entry contains reconstructed terms and roots . As such, the term(s) in this entry are not directly attested , but are hypothesized to have existed based on comparative evidence.

 Proto-Germanic
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Either from Proto-Indo-European *tuHsont- , *tuHsenti- (cf. also Proto-Slavic *tysǫti / *tysęti ( “ thousand ” ) and Lithuanian tūkstantis ), or – as Kroonen suggests – from Proto-Indo-European *tuHsdḱmto- , compounded from a prefix *tuHs- (“fat”, from *tewh₂- ) and *ḱm̥tóm ( “ hundred ” ) . [ 1 ] The latter form is effectively recorded as Old Norse þúshundrað ( “ thousand ” ) , combining hundrað ( “ hundred ” ) with a þús- prefix.

 Compare Italian milione from mille +‎ -one (“large thousand”).
