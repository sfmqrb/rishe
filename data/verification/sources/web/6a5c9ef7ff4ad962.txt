 Persian
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Middle Persian [Book Pahlavi needed] ( ʾcydhʾk' /⁠az(i)dahāg⁠/ ) , from Avestan 𐬀𐬲𐬌⸱𐬛𐬀𐬵𐬁𐬐𐬀 ( aži.dahāka , “ Azhi Dahaka ” ) . Spelling influenced by the etymologically unrelated Arabic, ضَحَّاك ( ḍaḥḥāk ) . Related to اژدها ( aždahā , “ dragon ” ) . Cognate with Armenian Աժդահակ ( Aždahak ) .

 Proper noun
 [ edit ] 

 ضحاک • ( zahhāk  / zahhâk ) ( Tajik spelling Заҳҳок ) 

 Zahhak ; antagonistic king in the Shahnameh 
 c. 977-1010 , ابوالقاسم فردوسی توسی [ Abū l-Qāsim Firdawsī Tūsī ], “Jamshid”, in Arthur George Warner and Edmond Warner, transl., شاهنامه [ šāhnāma , Shahnameh ]‎ [1] , published 1905 , The Story of Zahhák and his Father: جهانجوی را نام ضحاک بود
دلیر و سبگسار و ناباک بود Jahānjōy rā nām Zahhāk būd
Dilēr u sabuksār u nāpāk būd He had a son
Whom much he loved — Zahhak , a gallant prince,
But hasty.
