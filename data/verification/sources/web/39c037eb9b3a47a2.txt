 English

 English Wikipedia has an article on: pilaf 

 Wikipedia 

 Alternative forms
 [ edit ] 

 pilaff , pillaf , pillaff , pillaw , pilao , pilau , pilav , plov , polao , pulao , pulau 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Borrowed from Ottoman Turkish پلاو (modern Turkish pilav ), from Classical Persian پلاو ( pilāw ) , from Hindi पुलाव ( pulāv ) / Urdu پُلاؤ ( pulāo ) , from Sauraseni Prakrit *𑀧𑀼𑀮𑀸𑀕 ( *pulāga ) , from Sanskrit पुलाक ( pulāka ) , which is probably of Dravidian origin. [ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] Akin to Tamil புழுக்கு ( puḻukku , “ cooked rice ” ) . Doublet of pulao .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Australia ) IPA ( key ) : /pɪlɐːf/ 

 ( Received Pronunciation ) IPA ( key ) : /piːlɑːf/ 

 ( General American ) enPR : pee-lăf , IPA ( key ) : /piːlæf/ 

 Audio ( US ) : ( file ) 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 pilaf ( countable and uncountable , plural pilafs ) 

 A dish made by browning grain , typically rice , in oil and then cooking it with a seasoned broth , to which meat and/or vegetables may be added.
 1952 , Cothburn O’Neal, chapter 1, in Master of the World (Avon; N158 ), New York, N.Y.: Avon Books , published 1967 , →OCLC , book 1, page 17 : My spirits rose as I passed among the tents and savored the aroma of steaming pots of pilaf and the long skewers of shashlyk roasting over the open fires. 

 2007 March 9, “Great divide: The fight for America's heritage”, in The Independent ‎ [1] , archived from the original on 1 July 2022 : Charlie Vaughn, the tribal chairman, dismissed his critics at the opening as people who are "eating tofu and pilaf and sitting in Phoenix with their plasma-screen TVs".
