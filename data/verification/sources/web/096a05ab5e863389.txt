 English
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 From Middle English with , from Old English wiþ ( “ against, opposite, toward, with ” ) , from Proto-West Germanic *wiþi , a shortened form of Proto-Germanic *wiþrą ( “ against ” ) . 

 In Middle English, the word shifted to denote association rather than opposition, displacing Middle English mid ( “ with ” ) , from Old English mid ( “ with ” ) , from Proto-Germanic *midi ; an earlier model of this meaning shift exists in cognate Old Norse við ; elsewhere, the converse meaning shift is exemplified by Old South Arabian 𐩨𐩺𐩬 ( byn , “ between, amid ” ) spawning Old South Arabian 𐩨𐩬 ( bn , “ against ” ) and even likewise frequent reverse meaning 𐩨𐩬 ( bn , “ from ” ) . The adverb sense is probably a semantic loan from various other Germanic languages, such as German mit , Norwegian med , and Swedish med . [ 1 ]
