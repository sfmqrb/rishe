 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From earlier Classical Persian پِلَاوْ ( pilāw ) / پَلَاوْ ( palāw ) , borrowed from Hindustani ( Hindi पुलाव ( pulāv ) / Urdu پُلاؤ ( pulāo ) ), from Sauraseni Prakrit *𑀧𑀼𑀮𑀸𑀕 ( *pulāga ) , from Sanskrit पुलाक ( pulāka , “ lump of boiled rice ” ) .

 The term is said to be of Dravidian origin; compare Kannada ಪುೞ್ಗಿ ( puḻgi , “ a rice dish ” ) , Malayalam പുഴുങ്ങുക ( puḻuṅṅuka , “ to be boiling ” ) , Tamil புழுக்கல் ( puḻukkal , “ cooked rice ” ) , and Telugu పులగము ( pulagamu , “ a dish made with rice and lentils ” ) .

 Compare पुलाक ( pulāka ) 's inherited descendant Punjabi ਪੁਲਾਉ  / پُلاؤ ( pulāu ) .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 پلو • ( pulaw, palaw / polov, palov ) ( plural پلوها ( pulaw-hā, palaw-hā / polov-hâ, palov-hâ ) , Tajik spelling пулав , or палав ) 

 pilaf ( a dish made by browning grain , typically rice , in oil and then cooking it with a seasoned broth , to which meat and/or vegetables may be added )
