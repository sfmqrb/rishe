 English
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Greek ντολμάς ( ntolmás ) or its etymon, Turkish dolma , from Ottoman Turkish طولمه ( dolma ) , from طولمق ( dolmak , “ to get full, be filled ” ) . [ 1 ] [ 2 ] Thus, the word literally means “stuffed thing”.

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( General American ) IPA ( key ) : /ˈdoʊlmə/ 

 Audio ( Southern England ) : ( file ) 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 dolma ( plural dolmas or dolmades or dolmathes ) 

 Any of a family of stuffed vegetable dishes. The filling generally consists of rice, minced meat or grains, together with onion, herbs and spices.
 2026 , James Patterson and Chris Tebbetts, Dr. Zeus , pages 10-11 : "Twelve gyro supreme platters, six chicken souvlaki, four more with pork, six dolmas , six mezze platters, six Greek salads, eight baklava, and a side of tzatziki!" he said.

 dolma ( plural dolma - dolma )

 Turkish
 [ edit ] 

   dolma on Turkish Wikipedia

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( etymology 1 ) : IPA ( key ) : /doɫˈma/ 

 ( etymology 2 ) : IPA ( key ) : /ˈdoɫ.ma/ 

 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Inherited from Ottoman Turkish طولمه ( dolma ) ; by surface analysis , dol- ( “ to fill up ” ) +‎ -ma .

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 dolma ( definite accusative dolmayı , plural dolmalar ) 

 verbal noun of dolmak :
 Baraj suyla dolma ya başladı. ― The dam started filling up with water. 
 dolma , a dish made by filling a poultry bird, ribs of a lamb or vegetables with a rice -based stuffing mix
 Near-synonym: sarma 

 ( slang ) joint , blunt , fatty , a cigarette rolled with a mixture of marijuana and other recreational drugs
