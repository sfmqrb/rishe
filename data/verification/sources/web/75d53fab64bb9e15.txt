 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 From Proto-Indo-Iranian *spliHȷ́ʰā́ , from Proto-Indo-European *(s)pli(H)ǵʰḗn . [ 1 ] Cognate with Ancient Greek σπλήν ( splḗn ) (whence English spleen ), Latin lien , Avestan 𐬯𐬞𐬆𐬭𐬆𐬰𐬀𐬥 ( spər ə zan ) , Old Armenian փայծաղն ( pʻaycałn ) , Russian селезёнка ( selezjónka ) , Lithuanian blužnis , Persian اسپرز ( esporz ) .

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Vedic ) IPA ( key ) : /pliː.ɦɐ́n/ 

 ( Classical Sanskrit ) IPA ( key ) : /pl̪iː.ɦɐn̪/ 

 Noun
 [ edit ] 

 प्ली॒हन् • ( plīhán ) stem ,  m 

 the spleen 
 c. 1200 BCE – 1000 BCE , Atharvaveda 2.33.3 : हृदयात्ते परि क्लोम्नो हलीक्ष्णात्पार्श्वाभ्याम् ।
यक्ष्मं मतस्नाभ्यां प्लीह्नो यक्नस्ते वि वृहामसि ॥ hṛdayātte pari klomno halīkṣṇātpārśvābhyām.
yakṣmaṃ matasnābhyāṃ plīhno yaknaste vi vṛhāmasi. Forth from thy heart and from thy lungs, from thy gall-bladder and thy sides,
From kidneys, spleen and liver thy malady we uproot. 

 c. 700 BCE , Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa
