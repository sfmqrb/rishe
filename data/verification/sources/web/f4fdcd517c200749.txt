 Etymology 1
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Germanic *ubilaz 
 Proto-West Germanic *ubil 
 Old English yfel 
 Middle English yvel 
 English evil 

 From Middle English yvel , evel , ivel , uvel , from Old English yfel , from Proto-West Germanic *ubil , from Proto-Germanic *ubilaz , [ 1 ] from Proto-Indo-European *h₂up(h₁)élos , a deverbal derivative of *h₂wep(h₁)-, *h₂wop(h₁)- ( “ treat badly ” ) . See -le for the supposed suffix.

 Alternatively from *upélos ( “ evil ” , literally “ going over or beyond (acceptable limits) ” ) , from Proto-Indo-European *upo , *h₃ewp- ( “ down, up, over ” ) . [ 2 ] 

 Cognates

 Cognate with Dutch euvel ( “ evil ” ) , German übel ( “ bad, evil ” ) , German Low German övel ( “ evil ” ) , Luxembourgish iwwel ( “ queasy, nauseous; bad ” ) , Gothic 𐌿𐌱𐌹𐌻𐍃 ( ubils , “ bad, evil ” ) . Compare Old Irish fel ( “ bad, evil ” ) , from Proto-Celtic *uɸelos , [ 3 ] and Hittite 𒄷𒉿𒀊𒍣 ( huwapp- i , “ to mistreat, harass ” ) , 𒄷𒉿𒀊𒉺𒀸 ( huwappa- , “ evil, badness ” ) . [ 4 ]

 English evil 

 From Middle English yvele , yvel , ivel , from Old English yfele ( “ badly, evilly ” ) , a derivative of the adjective yfel ( “ bad, evil ” ) . Often reinterpreted as the noun in the later language (as in "to speak evil").
