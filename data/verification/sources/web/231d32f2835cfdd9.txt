 Latin
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology
 [ edit ] 

 Etymology tree

 Proto-Indo-European *weǵ- der. 
 Proto-Italic *wegēō ? 
 Latin vigeo 

 Uncertain. Perhaps a stative form from the Proto-Indo-European root *weǵ- ( “ to be lively ” ) , possibly via Proto-Italic *wegēō . De Vaan and Sihler relate the term to vegeō , which De Vaan argues is indicative of an original adjective *weg-o- ( “ active , alive , awake ” ) , which De Vaan suggests may be the source of the Latin term. [ 1 ] The *-i- is unexpected in the Latin form; De Vaan suspect this to be a vowel raising from original *-e- after the labial v- , as also seen in firmus and vitulus . [ 2 ] 

 Pronunciation
 [ edit ] 

 ( Classical Latin ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈwɪ.ɡe.oː] 

 ( modern Italianate Ecclesiastical ) IPA ( key ) : [ˈviː.d͡ʒe.o] 

 Verb
 [ edit ] 

 vigeō ( present infinitive vigēre , perfect active viguī ) ; second conjugation , no passive , no supine stem 

 to be vigorous or thriving ; thrive , flourish 
 Synonyms: polleo , possum , valeō , praevaleō , queō 
 Antonym: nequeō 
 29 BCE – 19 BCE , Vergilius , Aeneis 4.175 : Mōbilitāte viget , vīrēsque adquīrit eundō. [Rumor] thrives on swift action, and gains strength by going forth. 

 to be in honor , esteem or repute ; prosper 
 c. 117 CE , Tacitus , Annales 4.20 : neque tamen temperamenti egebat, cum aequabili auctoritate et gratia apud Tiberium viguerit nor however did he lack discretion, since he was held in esteem equally with authority and favour with Tiberius 

 to be alive , live
